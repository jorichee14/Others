\subsection{Collaboration Geometry in Multi-Agent Datasets}
\label{sec:related}

A collaborative-perception benchmark reports accuracy obtained under a
collaboration geometry---which nodes could see an object, how well, and from
what relative viewpoint---yet that geometry is seldom a released quantity.
Datasets differ along two axes: whether the geometry is deliberate, and whether
it is measured per observation or merely described. \emph{Paradigm-driven
multi-robot collection} makes it deliberate: S3E~\cite{s3e} names four
collaborative trajectory paradigms and grades each against loop-closure
opportunity with filled and open circles, and CU-Multi~\cite{cumulti} treats
inter-trajectory overlap as a controlled variable. The grading, however, is an
assertion about a run rather than a quantity attached to a frame, and the
overlap is obtained by re-running one platform four times, so no instantaneous
geometry exists to measure; both, moreover, collaborate between sensors at a
common height, leaving the elevated-to-ground asymmetry absent by construction.
\emph{Collaborative perception datasets} make it emergent: relative geometry
follows from how the collecting vehicles happened to drive, whether simulated
\cite{opv2v} or recorded on public roads \cite{v2v4real}, or from sensor
placement and the ego's approach at an instrumented site \cite{dairv2x}.
Per-frame poses are necessarily released, feature warping depending on them, so
relative placement is recoverable; what is absent is the condition itself---no
field states whether the ego's view of an object was blocked, whether a
partner's was clear, or at what angle the two observed it, and in the recorded
sets no dense scene model accompanies the release from which to reconstruct it.
Splits are cut by town, road type, or scene, never by geometry, and where
geometry does enter it enters as a nuisance to be corrected: pose error is
modelled, injected, and compensated, while the geometry that error perturbs is
not itself an axis of evaluation. \emph{Single-agent benchmarks} come closest to
releasing the condition---occlusion and truncation flags \cite{kitti}, binned
visibility \cite{nuscenes}, and per-annotation return counts that exclude
objects from scoring or assign them to a lower difficulty tier
\cite{nuscenes,waymo}---but compute them for one ego and collapse them into a
single tier mixing occlusion, truncation, and a range proxy, so that an object
the ego could not see is no longer distinguishable from one it could see but
returned too little evidence for. Measured collaboration geometry itself appears
only in reduced form: an occlusion degree over roadside LiDAR at a fixed vantage
between sensors of common height, summarised per dataset rather than per frame
\cite{inscope}; or blockage measured at scale but inferred from received power
\cite{deepsense6g}, so that the label and the signal it explains are not
independent.

The field thus faces a trade: geometry that is deliberate is described rather
than measured, and geometry that is measured is computed for a single ego,
summarised away, or derived from the very signal it is meant to explain. Our
dataset breaks this trade. Trajectory paradigms make the geometry deliberate;
occlusion, visibility and evidence sufficiency kept distinct, aspect angle,
co-visibility, and Fresnel clearance are then measured per (frame, node,
object); each label is validated against a measurement that did not enter its
computation---sensor returns within the annotated box for occlusion, RSSI for
link state; and all of it is released as columns keyed to the recordings, so
that an evaluation can be conditioned on the situation that produced it. To our
knowledge, no existing collaborative dataset provides this combination. The
contribution is the axis and its validation rather than scale: the release is
smaller than every dataset named above.
