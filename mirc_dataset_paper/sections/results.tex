\section{Results}
\label{sec:findings}

Three observations follow from Table~\ref{tab:seq}, each of a kind an aggregate
metric cannot produce.

\paragraph{Range, not occlusion, is the binding constraint.}
The condition the collection was designed around is not the one that limits the
team. Occlusion rescue occurs on 0.10 and 0.02 of ego-in-FoV observations,
range rescue on 0.29 and 0.39. Stratified by ego--object range, rescue rates are
near zero below \SI{5}{\metre} and rise to \numrange{0.5}{0.7} beyond it, and
the mechanism is visible in \texttt{mobile\_2}'s per-range statistics: across
the \SIrange{2}{5}{\metre}, \SIrange{5}{10}{\metre}, and
\SIrange{10}{15}{\metre} bins its fraction \textit{visible} stays at
\numrange{0.96}{1.00} while its fraction \textit{observed} collapses from
\num{0.85} to \num{0.00}. The geometry admits sight of the object throughout;
the sensor stops returning it past the first bin. A release reporting
line-of-sight geometry alone would characterise this team as having
near-complete coverage. The effect was not staged, which makes it stronger
evidence than the occlusion episodes that were.

\paragraph{The evidence definition changes the rate by a factor of 36.}
\texttt{mobile\_1}'s range-rescue rate is \num{0.008} under Ouster LiDAR returns
and \num{0.29} under ZED stereo depth, for the same trajectory, scene, ray
casts, annotations, and occlusion labels; only the floor and the calibrated
range cap in~\eqref{eq:observed} changed. A collaboration rate reported without
its evidence source is therefore not reproducible to better than a factor of
tens, and rates computed under different sensing budgets are not comparable.
The per-modality evidence counts are released per (frame, node, object) so that
any such rate can be recomputed under a stated definition.

\paragraph{Viewpoint complementarity is positional, not elevational.}
The same infrastructure node, in the same pose, is a complementary viewpoint
for one mobile node and a redundant one for the other: median $\beta$ of
$149^\circ$ with $\beta > 90^\circ$ on 67\,\% of frames when \texttt{mobile\_1}
is ego, $29^\circ$ and 33\,\% when \texttt{mobile\_2} is. The elevation offset
is identical in both cases; what differs is where the ego stood relative to the
infrastructure and the object. Height confers persistence and an unobstructed
downward line, but not by itself the wide baseline from which fusion benefits.
Reporting $\Delta h$, as infrastructure-augmented datasets conventionally do,
does not characterise the collaboration geometry; $\beta$ conditioned on the
ego does.

\paragraph{Limits of the present evidence.}
Two annotated static objects make $C$ a three-valued quantity and every rate
derived from it coarse; the occlusion claims rest on 9 and 8 episodes, not on
\num{733} frames, since frames within an episode are not independent; and one
\SI{156}{\second} run at one site supports no site- or platform-general claim.
Doppler diversity is undefined without motion, handover is under-sampled with
one infrastructure node, and the link-state paradigms are computed but not yet
folded into the classifier. Both ego assignments of one recording control for
platform but not trajectory; paired repeats with a single factor varied are the
stronger control and are not yet collected. The additions that make every
paradigm computable are scripted moving participants, full annotation of a
bounded region, a second infrastructure node, and radar on \texttt{mobile\_2}.
