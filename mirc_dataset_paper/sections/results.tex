The run was planned as co-observation. Measured, it is co-observation in
74\,\% of ego-in-FoV observations with \texttt{mobile\_1} as ego and 77\,\%
with \texttt{mobile\_2}; the remainder is occlusion, handover, and

anyway. This is the design check described in
Section~\ref{sec:paradigms}: a trajectory written for one condition delivered
that condition about three frames in four, and the dataset says which frames
were the other one. A qualitative grading would have reported this run as
co-observation and stopped.

undefined without moving targets, and the run contains none; an empty cell and
a zero are different claims.

Predicted geometric occlusion is negatively correlated with the evidence
actually returned, on both of \texttt{mobile\_1}'s independent modalities and
at comparable strength, which is what a correct occlusion label should look
like against noisy sensors. Predicted link clearance is strongly positively
correlated with measured RSSI on both mobile nodes, and the clearance-derived
LOS/NLOS split separates median RSSI by \SI{8}{\decibel} and \SI{11}{\decibel}
respectively---a purely geometric predicate reproducing a radio measurement it
never saw.

\subsection{Findings}

\paragraph{Finding 1: range, not occlusion, limits this team.}
The condition the collection was designed around is not the condition that
binds. Occlusion rescue---the ego blocked and a helper covering it---occurs on
0.10 and 0.02 of ego-in-FoV observations. Range rescue, where the ego has a
clear line to the object and still returns too little evidence to detect it
while a partner does not, occurs on 0.29 and 0.39. Stratified by ego--object
range, rescue rates are near zero below \SI{5}{\metre} and rise to
\numrange{0.5}{0.7} beyond it. The mechanism is visible directly in
\texttt{mobile\_2}'s per-range statistics: across the \SIrange{2}{5}{\metre},
\SIrange{5}{10}{\metre}, and \SIrange{10}{15}{\metre} bins its fraction
\emph{visible} stays at \numrange{0.96}{1.00} while its fraction
\emph{observed} collapses \num{0.85} $\rightarrow$ \num{0.00} $\rightarrow$
\num{0.00}. Geometry says it can see the object throughout; its sensor stops
returning the object entirely past the first bin.

This is the distinction of~\eqref{eq:visible} versus~\eqref{eq:observed} doing
work. A dataset that reports only line-of-sight geometry would describe this
team as having near-perfect coverage. Nothing about the effect was staged,
which makes it stronger evidence than the occlusion episodes that were.

\paragraph{Finding 2: the choice of evidence source changes the answer by
$36\times$.}
\texttt{mobile\_1}'s range-rescue rate is \num{0.008} when its evidence is
Ouster LiDAR returns and \num{0.29} when it is ZED stereo depth---same
trajectory, same scene, same ray casts, same annotations, same occlusion
labels. Only the definition of ``the ego has enough evidence'' changed, via the
modality's floor and its calibrated range cap. A collaboration rate reported
without naming its evidence source is therefore not reproducible to better than
a factor of tens, and rates computed under different sensing budgets are not
comparable at all. We release the per-modality evidence counts per (frame,
node, object) so that any such rate can be recomputed under a stated
definition, and we report Table~\ref{tab:seq}'s range-rescue column under the
RGB-D definition explicitly.

\paragraph{Finding 3: viewpoint complementarity is positional, not
elevational.}
The same infrastructure node, in the same pose, is a complementary viewpoint
for one mobile node and a redundant one for the other. With \texttt{mobile\_1}
as ego the median aspect angle to infrastructure is $149^\circ$ and
$\beta > 90^\circ$ on 67\,\% of frames; with \texttt{mobile\_2} as ego it is
$29^\circ$ and 33\,\%. Elevation is identical in both cases
($\Delta h = \SI{1.53}{\metre}$, IQR \SIrange{1.52}{1.54}{\metre}). What
differs is where the ego stood relative to the infrastructure and the object.
Height buys persistence and an unobstructed downward line; it does not by
itself buy the wide baseline that fusion benefits from. Reporting
$\Delta h$---as infrastructure-augmented datasets typically do---does not
characterise the collaboration geometry, and $\beta$ conditioned on the ego
does.
