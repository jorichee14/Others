\section{Dataset Design and Characterization}
\label{sec:collection}

\subsection{Environments and Roles}
\label{sec:environments}

Data collection was conducted on the Guangfu Campus of National Yang Ming Chiao
Tung University (NYCU), at indoor and outdoor sites. Site selection was governed
by three factors, each associated with a quantity the release reports per frame
rather than with a qualitative descriptor. \textit{Viewpoint}: whether the site
admits vantages displaced from one another---an elevated infrastructure position,
and mobile nodes able to separate in the ground plane---characterised by the
range of aspect angles $\beta$ realisable between an ego and a helper.
\textit{Appearance}: illumination, texture, and clutter density, which determine
the range beyond which $\mathrm{visible}(i)$ ceases to imply
$\mathrm{observed}(i)$. \textit{Radio propagation}: whether structure intervenes
between a node and the access point, characterised by first-Fresnel-zone
clearance. Each site is surveyed and mapped once as a dense point cloud, with
all node poses expressed in a single anchored map frame, so that occlusion,
co-visibility, and link geometry are evaluated against one geometric
representation.

One mobile agent is designated the \textit{ego} and is viewpoint limited by
design, either by trajectory---routed such that structure intervenes between it
and the annotated objects---or by sensing budget, carrying the shorter-range
depth sensor; the second is the \textit{partner}. Ego assignment alternates
across runs, so that a reported condition is attributable to the geometry rather
than to a platform. Infrastructure nodes are never assigned the ego role, their
function being the elevated, persistent vantage no mobile node can sustain;
since comparable datasets collaborate between sensors at a common height
(Section~\ref{sec:related}), assigning one as ego would reinstate the symmetry
this collection is designed to break.

The sequence characterised below was recorded at the MIRC site, an indoor
laboratory comprising one surveyed elevated camera--radar mast, a
\SI{5.75}{\giga\hertz} access point positioned behind furniture, and static
seating that constitutes the occluding structure.

\subsection{Collaboration Geometry}
\label{sec:paradigms}

A paradigm specifies a trajectory geometry together with the collaboration
condition that geometry is intended to induce. The vocabulary is that of
paradigm-driven multi-robot collection; what is added here is that each paradigm
is a predicate over per-frame quantities, computed from the map, the
annotations, and the node poses, and released with the recordings rather than
asserted of a run.

Consider one annotated object at one frame. Let $e$ denote the ego and $j$ range
over the remaining nodes. For node $i$, let $\omega_i \in [0,1]$ denote the
fraction of rays cast from $i$'s sensor origin to the object surface that are
blocked by mapped structure, $r_i$ the range to the object, and $n_i$ the
evidence returns falling within the annotated box---LiDAR points, depth points,
or projected pixel area, according to the node's primary modality. With
$\tau = 0.5$,
%
\begin{align}
\mathrm{visible}(i) &\equiv \text{in FoV} \;\wedge\; \omega_i < \tau,
\label{eq:visible}\\
\mathrm{observed}(i) &\equiv \mathrm{visible}(i) \;\wedge\; n_i \ge n_{\min,i}
\;\wedge\; r_i \le r_{\max,i}.
\label{eq:observed}
\end{align}
%
Visibility is a geometric condition: an unobstructed line exists. Observation is
an evidence condition: the sensor returned support sufficient for a detection,
within the range at which that modality still returns anything. Both have
single-agent precedent as annotation attributes and evaluation filters
(Section~\ref{sec:related}); here they are computed for every node and kept
distinct, so that an object the ego could not see remains separable from one it
could see but for which it returned insufficient evidence. That separation
carries the analysis of Section~\ref{sec:findings}.

A node $j \ne e$ is a \textit{helper} if $\mathrm{visible}(j)$, and the best
helper $j^\star$ is that of lowest $\omega$. The aspect angle $\beta$ is
subtended at the object between the rays to $e$ and to $j^\star$, so $\beta$
near $0^\circ$ indicates a redundant viewpoint and $\beta > 90^\circ$ a
complementary one. Each frame further records the co-visibility fraction $C$,
being objects visible to at least two nodes over those visible to at least one,
and, for each link, the minimum first-Fresnel-zone clearance against the same
occupancy grid, normalised by the Fresnel radius; $\mathrm{clear}(a,b) < 0.6$ is
the obstructed-link criterion. Table~\ref{tab:paradigms} composes these into the
paradigms, evaluated in order so that a condition combining occlusion with a
degraded link is reported as such rather than absorbed into the plain occlusion
class. There $v_o$ is the object speed, $v_{r,i}$ its radial velocity relative
to node $i$, and $v_{\min} = k\lambda / (2 N_c T_c)$ with $k \approx 1.5$ the
Doppler resolution floor of the radar.

Labels are descriptive rather than prescriptive. A label is assigned per frame
from measured metrics, not declared once per run, so a sequence intended as
co-observation contains occlusion frames wherever the geometry produced them.
Evaluation strata are consequently cut by the raw metrics and never by the
label: performance is reported against $\omega$, $\beta$, $r$, $C$, and link
clearance directly. Each run additionally records an \texttt{intended\_paradigm}
in its metadata, and the agreement between intended and measured quantifies how
reliably a trajectory plan produces the condition for which it was written.

The per-frame quantities are keyed by (\texttt{t\_ns}, \texttt{node},
\texttt{obj\_id}), so that any evaluation can be joined to the condition under
which each observation was made. They admit three uses. They serve as strata,
accuracy being reported within bands of $\omega$, $r$, $\beta$, $C$, or link
clearance rather than averaged over the mixture a recording happens to contain.
They serve as a join between sensing and communication, link state and
perception need being expressed on a common clock, so that the value of a
transmission can be assessed at the frame at which it would have been made. And
they serve as an objective, aspect angle, co-visibility, and clearance being
quantities against which a viewpoint, transmission, or placement policy can be
scored directly.

The geometric pipeline is frozen across sequences: $\tau = 0.5$; occupancy voxel
\SI{10}{\centi\metre}; 200 rays per object, node, and frame; statistical outlier
removal at 20 neighbours and $2\sigma$; a voxel occupied only at $\ge 8$ points
and a ray blocked only on two consecutive occupied voxels; occluder hits within
\SI{0.6}{\metre} of a sensor origin and occupancy within \SI{0.7}{\metre} of
either link endpoint disregarded, both nodes and the access point being
themselves present in the map; evidence floors of 1 LiDAR point, 50 depth
points, and 400\,px$^2$; range caps of \SI{20}{\metre} (Ouster),
\SI{4.5}{\metre} (ZED), \SI{5}{\metre} (RealSense), and \SI{20}{\metre}
(infrastructure pixels), each calibrated from the range at which median evidence
falls to its floor; obstructed link below $0.6\,r_1$; carrier frequency read per
frame from the WiFi status stream. The density threshold, consecutive-hit test,
and endpoint clearances are workarounds for map noise and for fixed nodes
appearing as mapped structure; they are stated because they affect the reported
values, and a survey-grade map should permit their removal.

\subsection{Sequence Characterization}
\label{sec:descriptor}

Each sequence is summarised by one row per ego assignment in four blocks of
frozen columns (Table~\ref{tab:seq}). The \textit{design} block records the
sequence, site, intended paradigm, any stress factor, and which node is ego. The
\textit{geometry} block reports the median $C$, the median $\beta$ to
infrastructure, the proportion of observations at $\beta > 90^\circ$, and the
count of occlusion episodes, an episode being a contiguous interval of
$\neg\,\mathrm{visible}(e)$ on one object lasting at least \SI{0.5}{\second}.
The \textit{rescue} block reports the proportion of ego-in-FoV observations in
which a partner supplies what the ego does not: \textit{occlusion rescue}, the
ego blocked and a helper not; \textit{range rescue}, the ego holding an
unobstructed line but returning insufficient evidence,
$\mathrm{visible}(e) \wedge \neg\,\mathrm{observed}(e)$, while a helper
observes; and \textit{Doppler rescue}, the ego's radar blind to motion another
node's radar resolves. The final block gives the fraction of frames on which the
ego--AP link is obstructed or degraded, and the median number of annotated
objects in view.

The run was planned as co-observation. Measured, it is co-observation in
74\,\% of ego-in-FoV observations with \texttt{mobile\_1} as ego and 77\,\% with
\texttt{mobile\_2}; the remainder are occlusion, handover, and unstructured
frames that the plan did not call for and that the labels record regardless. A
trajectory written for one condition delivered it in approximately three frames
out of four, and the release identifies which frames were the fourth. Doppler
diversity is left empty rather than reported as zero: the predicate is undefined
in the absence of moving targets, and this sequence contains none.

\subsection{Validation of the Released Labels}
\label{sec:results}

The occlusion and link-state labels are geometric predictions derived from a
map, and each is therefore checked against a measured signal that did not enter
its computation (Table~\ref{tab:validation}). Predicted occlusion is negatively
correlated with the evidence actually returned, on both of \texttt{mobile\_1}'s
independent modalities and at comparable strength, which is the expected
signature of a correct occlusion label against noisy sensors. Predicted link
clearance is strongly positively correlated with measured RSSI on both mobile
nodes, and the clearance-derived partition separates median RSSI by
\SI{8}{\decibel} and \SI{11}{\decibel}: a purely geometric predicate reproducing
a radio measurement that did not enter it.

\subsection{What the Measurement Shows}
\label{sec:findings}

\paragraph{Range, not occlusion, is the binding constraint.}
The condition the collection was designed around is not the one that limits the
team. Occlusion rescue occurs on 0.10 and 0.02 of ego-in-FoV observations,
range rescue on 0.29 and 0.39; stratified by ego--object range, rescue rates are
near zero below \SI{5}{\metre} and rise to \numrange{0.5}{0.7} beyond it. The
mechanism appears directly in \texttt{mobile\_2}'s per-range statistics: across
the \SIrange{2}{5}{\metre}, \SIrange{5}{10}{\metre}, and
\SIrange{10}{15}{\metre} bins its fraction \textit{visible} remains at
\numrange{0.96}{1.00} while its fraction \textit{observed} collapses
\num{0.85} $\rightarrow$ \num{0.00} $\rightarrow$ \num{0.00}. The geometry
admits sight of the object throughout; the sensor ceases to return it past the
first bin. This is the separation of~\eqref{eq:visible}
from~\eqref{eq:observed} doing work---a release reporting line-of-sight geometry
alone would characterise this team as having near-complete coverage---and the
effect was not staged, which makes it stronger evidence than the occlusion
episodes that were.

\paragraph{The choice of evidence source changes the rate by a factor of 36.}
\texttt{mobile\_1}'s range-rescue rate is \num{0.008} under Ouster LiDAR returns
and \num{0.29} under ZED stereo depth, for the same trajectory, scene, ray
casts, annotations, and occlusion labels; only the definition of sufficient
evidence changed, through the modality's floor and calibrated range cap. A
collaboration rate reported without its evidence source is therefore not
reproducible to better than a factor of tens, and rates computed under different
sensing budgets are not comparable. Per-modality evidence counts are released
per (frame, node, object) so that any such rate may be recomputed under a stated
definition.

\paragraph{Viewpoint complementarity is positional, not elevational.}
The same infrastructure node, in the same pose, constitutes a complementary
viewpoint for one mobile node and a redundant one for the other: with
\texttt{mobile\_1} as ego the median aspect angle is $149^\circ$ and
$\beta > 90^\circ$ on 67\,\% of frames, with \texttt{mobile\_2} as ego
$29^\circ$ and 33\,\%. The elevation offset is identical in both cases; what
differs is the ego's position relative to the infrastructure and the object.
Height confers persistence and an unobstructed downward line, but not by itself
the wide baseline from which fusion benefits. Reporting $\Delta h$, as
infrastructure-augmented datasets conventionally do, does not characterise the
collaboration geometry, whereas $\beta$ conditioned on the ego does.
