## Systems: what each multi-agent radiance-field / Gaussian SLAM system does (architecture, sensors, merging, loop closure, data, results, limitations)

### Takeaway
From 2023 to 2026 the field went from CP-SLAM (2 agents, neural points, RGB-D) to MAGiC-SLAM and MAC-Ego3D (CVPR 2025, centralized or asynchronous, RGB-D), then GRAND-SLAM (outdoor, Kimera-Multi data, RGB-D), CoMA-SLAM (AAAI 2026, distributed, 2D surfels, RGB-D) and Coko-SLAM (2026, compact submaps). In 2026 came the first RGB-only or RGB+IMU variants (MAGS-SLAM, CGS-SLAM). Almost all of them are evaluated on Multiagent Replica and AriaMultiagent, and they assume **one camera type per team**, mostly RGB-D. No multi-agent Gaussian SLAM system uses LiDAR as an input sensor. HAMMER is the only system explicitly built around heterogeneous devices.

### Cited Findings
**CP-SLAM (NeurIPS 2023), Hu et al.**
- A collaborative neural point-based SLAM system. The authors state these limitations: it needs "considerable GPU resources to operate multiple image sequences"; it fills unobserved regions less well than feature-grid methods, because neural points encode context only within a fixed-radius sphere around observed surfaces; and rendering-based relative-pose optimization in loop closure "may be inaccurate for large viewpoint changes thus leading to drifting of map fusion" — [CP-SLAM PDF (NeurIPS)](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7c10e259c7e56fa218ee03d9ae7d728e-Paper-Conference.pdf) (snippet)
- According to the MAGiC-SLAM paper, CP-SLAM is restricted to two agents and is slow — [MAGiC-SLAM CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yugay_MAGiC-SLAM_Multi-Agent_Gaussian_Globally_Consistent__SLAM_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf) (snippet)

**MAGiC-SLAM (CVPR 2025), Yugay et al.**
- Centralized. Each agent processes one limited-size submap of 3D Gaussians and sends it to a server after each submap is finished. The server does loop detection and pose-graph optimization and fuses submaps into a global map. It uses a "rigidly deformable" 3D Gaussian representation for speed. Input is RGB-D. It is evaluated on synthetic (Multiagent Replica) and real (Aria) data — [MAGiC-SLAM CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yugay_MAGiC-SLAM_Multi-Agent_Gaussian_Globally_Consistent__SLAM_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf); [arXiv 2411.16785](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.16785v1) (snippet)
- GRAND-SLAM reports that MAGiC-SLAM failed partway through the Agent-2 traverse of the Kimera-Multi "Outside 2" team and "fails to consistently find correct loop closures" outdoors — [GRAND-SLAM arXiv 2506.18885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18885) (snippet, a competitor's claim)
- A later paper describes MAGiC-SLAM as centralized, with geometric distortions/inconsistencies and limited scalability — [search snippet via AAAI CoMA-SLAM / follow-ups](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/37283/41245) (snippet; this is a competitor's critique)

**MAC-Ego3D (CVPR 2025), Xu, Xue, Zhao, Pan, Scherer, Huang**
- Uses intra-agent Gaussian consensus (spatial coherence within an agent) plus asynchronous inter-agent consensus (alignment of local maps). It runs in parallel and in real time. Input is RGB-D. The authors claim about 15x faster inference, much lower ego-motion error, and RGB PSNR gains of 4 to 10 dB — [CVPR 2025 page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Xu_MAC-Ego3D_Multi-Agent_Gaussian_Consensus_for_Real-Time_Collaborative_Ego-Motion_and_Photorealistic_CVPR_2025_paper.html) (snippet)
- On Multiagent Replica it reports 40.04 dB PSNR against 29.33 dB for CP-SLAM, and an average ATE of about 0.13–0.14 cm against about 0.9–1.2 cm for CP-SLAM, about 1.9–2.0 cm for ORB-SLAM3 and about 3.6 cm for Swarm-SLAM. The inter-agent communication interval is 150 frames on Replica and 60 on 7-Scenes — [arXiv 2412.09723](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09723v1) (snippet)
- I found no stated limitations in the snippets.

**GRAND-SLAM (arXiv 2506.18885, Jun 2025), Thomas, Sonawalla, Rose, How (MIT)**
- RGB-D multi-agent Gaussian SLAM. It builds submaps of 3D Gaussians incrementally, tracks implicitly by local optimization over submaps, and adds inter- and intra-robot loop closures (coarse-to-fine) to a pose graph. The authors call it "the first method to scalably integrate inter- and intra-robot loop closures" in multi-agent RGB-D GS SLAM, and say prior work was limited to small indoor scenes — [arXiv 2506.18885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18885) (snippet)
- Reported results: 28% higher PSNR than existing methods on Replica; 91% lower multi-agent tracking error and better rendering on the outdoor Kimera-Multi dataset. On Multiagent Replica it is "consistent with or marginally outperforms" MAGiC-SLAM — [arXiv 2506.18885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18885) (snippet)
- Absolute outdoor PSNR/depth numbers and the limitations section were not visible in the snippets.

**CoMA-SLAM (AAAI 2026), Su, Chen et al. (pi-lab)**
- Billed as "the first distributed multi-agent Gaussian SLAM framework". It uses 2D Gaussian surfels (it builds on the authors' GauS-SLAM) with a robust initialisation, intra- and inter-agent loop closure, distributed keyframe optimisation and submap-centric updates. Input is RGB-D. It is evaluated on Replica-multiagent and Aria-multiagent, and the authors claim better pose, rendering and geometry than the state of the art — [AAAI paper page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37283); [Gitee code](https://gitee.com/pi-lab/CoMA-SLAM) (snippet)
- One snippet says that avoiding transmission to a central server "reduces communication bandwidth by 99.8% compared to centralized approaches" — [AAAI paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37283) (snippet only; not verified)

**Coko-SLAM, "Compact Keyframe-Optimized Multi-Agent Gaussian Splatting SLAM" (arXiv 2604.00804, 2026), Monica M. Q. Li et al.**
- Multi-agent RGB-D 3DGS SLAM. Agents send only keyframe feature vectors, camera poses and pure 3D-Gaussian submaps to the server, with no point clouds or images. Inter-robot loop closure merges submaps without images or initial relative poses. Evaluation follows the MAGiC-SLAM protocol (Multiagent Replica: four two-agent sequences; plus Aria) — [arXiv 2604.00804](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00804) (snippet)
- The authors claim 85–95% less transmitted data than state-of-the-art methods, submap transmission in "several seconds rather than minutes" compared with MAGiC-SLAM, and rendering better than prior methods. Table 4 lists total data transmitted and submap sizes. The snippet says CP-SLAM and MAC-Ego3D have smaller submaps on Aria but render worse. Code: github.com/lemonci/coko-slam — [arXiv 2604.00804](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00804); [awesomepapers listing](https://awesomepapers.io/robotics/papers/2604.00804) (snippet; Table 4 values not seen)

**MAGS-SLAM (arXiv 2605.10760, 2026), Zhihao Cao et al.**
- Monocular, RGB-only multi-agent 3DGS SLAM. Agents build local monocular Gaussian submaps and send "compact submap summaries" instead of raw observations or dense maps. A Sim(3) submap pose graph is augmented with geometric **and photometric** residuals, a spatial-extent gate and adaptive edge invalidation. Loop verification is geometry- and appearance-aware, and fusion is occupancy-aware and asynchronous (borrowed Gaussians are deduplicated against each target agent's occupied/free voxels). It introduces a collaborative GS benchmark. On ReplicaMultiagent, AriaMultiagent and ETH3D it claims tracking competitive with RGB-D collaborative methods and comparable or better rendering — [arXiv 2605.10760](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10760v1) (snippet)
- The snippets did not say whether the "photometric" consistency includes exposure or colour-response compensation between agents.

**CGS-SLAM (arXiv 2608.26868, 2026), de Ambrogi, Chetouani, Nguyen, Chateigner**
- A hybrid system: decentralized tracking on each agent and centralized submap alignment. It uses only RGB and inertial data on smartphones. IMU is the motion prior and Depth Pro metric monocular depth gives scale. The authors claim scale-consistent reconstruction and accurate global alignment — [Hugging Face listing](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26868); [radiancefields summary](https://radiancefields.com/papers/cgs-slam-collaborative-gaussian-splatting-based-slam-for-multi-agent-reconstruction) (snippet; no numbers or limitations seen)

**HAMMER (RA-L 2025), Yu, Chen, Schwager (Stanford MSL)**
- Server-based, over ROS. It takes streaming image and depth data from a heterogeneous team (robots plus Aria glasses), each running its own on-board SLAM in unaligned frames. A one-time RGB-image-based metric frame alignment works across "devices with different image sensors and SLAM algorithms". It then trains a semantic 3DGS (CLIP-distilled) map online and "adjusts automatically for variations in image pre-processing among different devices". It claims 2x higher-fidelity maps than baselines and supports semantic goal-conditioned navigation — [arXiv 2501.14147](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14147v1); [Stanford MSL](https://msl.stanford.edu/bibliography/yu_hammer_2025.html) (snippet)

**Other related systems**
- MCN-SLAM (arXiv 2506.18678): multi-agent collaborative neural SLAM with a hybrid implicit representation — [arXiv 2506.18678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18678) (title/snippet only)
- Di-NeRF (RA-L 2024, Asadi, Zareinia, Saeedi): distributed NeRF using consensus ADMM. Robots exchange NeRF parameters (not raw data) over a mesh network, jointly refine relative poses, and support fully-connected, circular and ring topologies — [arXiv 2402.01485](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01485v2); [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10218660) (snippet)
- SIREN: initialisation-free registration of multi-robot GSplat maps using semantic matching, then coarse Gaussian-to-Gaussian alignment, then photometric refinement — [arXiv 2502.06519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06519) (snippet)
- DGOMapping: real-time multi-agent mapping with 4D Gaussian splatting. It uses a "growth–forgetting–cleanup" evolution under multi-agent asynchrony — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13306729/) (snippet)
- F3DGS: federated 3DGS for decentralized multi-agent world modelling — [arXiv 2604.01605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01605) (title only)
- CoMo3R-SLAM (arXiv 2605.30488): collaborative monocular dense SLAM with learned 3D priors for outdoor multi-robot systems — [arXiv 2605.30488](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30488) (title only; not necessarily GS)
- Survey: "A Survey on Collaborative SLAM with 3D Gaussian Splatting" (arXiv 2510.23988). It classifies systems as centralized or distributed. It names as core challenges global consistency, communication management and "fusing data from heterogeneous sources", and it flags lifelong mapping and Sim2Real as open directions. It says sharing large GS maps is a bottleneck under limited bandwidth, with compact representations and asynchronous fusion as mitigations — [arXiv 2510.23988](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23988v1); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397006354_A_Survey_on_Collaborative_SLAM_with_3D_Gaussian_Splatting) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Sensor assumptions: CP-SLAM, MAGiC-SLAM, MAC-Ego3D, GRAND-SLAM, CoMA-SLAM and Coko-SLAM are RGB-D. MAGS-SLAM is monocular RGB. CGS-SLAM is RGB+IMU. HAMMER takes RGB+depth from mixed devices. **None ingests LiDAR.** The benchmarks (Multiagent Replica, AriaMultiagent) have one camera model per sequence set, so the evaluation protocols never test cross-camera colour or intrinsics differences. This is inferred from the dataset descriptions; I did not confirm it for every paper.
- Architectures: centralized or server-based (MAGiC, Coko, HAMMER, CGS's alignment step), asynchronous or consensus (MAC-Ego3D), distributed (CoMA-SLAM, Di-NeRF). Scale: everything is room-scale on Replica/Aria, except GRAND-SLAM, which uses outdoor campus-scale Kimera-Multi RGB-D streams.
- Loop closure: rendering- or registration-based (CP-SLAM), feature or keyframe-descriptor based (Coko), and pose-graph with coarse-to-fine registration (GRAND). Every reported weakness concerns large viewpoint changes or outdoor scale.

### Gaps
- The full-text limitations sections of GRAND-SLAM, CoMA-SLAM, MAGS-SLAM, CGS-SLAM and Coko-SLAM could not be read because arxiv.org was blocked.
- I could not confirm whether HAMMER's "image pre-processing" adjustment is a learned colour or exposure model, or only resizing and intrinsics handling.
- The MACIM paper (multi-agent collaborative implicit mapping) did not surface in search.

## Heterogeneity: different cameras per agent, mixed LiDAR+camera teams, appearance harmonisation across agents and sessions

### Takeaway
Cross-camera appearance harmonisation for 3DGS is well studied for **single-rig multi-camera driving** (MC-Gaussian, MCSplat, bilateral grids, affine colour transforms). It has **not been carried into multi-agent or multi-session GS SLAM**. In the multi-agent space, only HAMMER (device pre-processing differences) and MAGS-SLAM (photometric residuals in the pose graph) touch it. Mixed LiDAR-inertial plus camera multi-robot Gaussian SLAM was not found. LiDAR-inertial-visual GS SLAM exists only for single robots.

### Cited Findings
- HAMMER handles different image sensors and SLAM stacks across devices and "adjusts automatically for variations in image pre-processing among different devices" — [arXiv 2501.14147](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14147v1) (snippet)
- MAGS-SLAM adds photometric residuals to its Sim(3) submap pose graph and uses appearance-aware loop verification — [arXiv 2605.10760](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10760v1) (snippet)
- MC-Gaussian (multi-camera autonomous driving) tackles "the color inconsistency among different cameras" with pose-conditioned colour correction — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-22264-0_30) (snippet)
- MCSplat (arXiv 2610.25345, 2026) targets cross-camera photometric inconsistency from exposure, ISP and illumination. It builds a canonical appearance space from depth-guided multi-camera correspondences and restores each camera's look with a multi-scale bilateral-grid transformer, so that colour differences do not leak into geometry — [arXiv 2610.25345](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25345) (snippet)
- Robust Gaussian Splatting (Darmon et al.) models per-camera colour changes with an affine colour transform — [arXiv 2404.04211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04211) (snippet)
- DAVIGS models appearance per image rather than per Gaussian. Without this, Gaussians "tend to adjust their colors" instead of the appearance model absorbing the variation — [arXiv 2501.10788](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10788) (snippet)
- Gaussian-DK uses a camera-response module over shared Gaussians — [arXiv 2408.09130](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09130v1) (snippet)
- MCGS-SLAM (multi-camera GS SLAM, single rig) targets inter-camera **scale** consistency with joint depth–scale alignment, not colour — [arXiv 2509.14191](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14191v1) (snippet)
- Single-robot LiDAR-inertial-camera GS SLAM exists. RMGS-SLAM (arXiv 2604.12942) runs loop closure on the Gaussian map via Gaussian-GICP plus a pose graph — [arXiv 2604.12942](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12942v1). Gaussian-LIC2 — [alphaxiv 2507.04004](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2507.04004). LVI-GS — [arXiv 2411.02703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02703) (snippets)
- A search for multi-robot, LiDAR-inertial, Gaussian map merging found no paper combining all three. Only LiDAR-only merging work came up (LEMON-Mapping 2505.10018, Commerge, UAV-MapFusion 2606.26928, MM-LINS 2503.19506) — [LEMON-Mapping](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10018); [Commerge](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/408047139_Commerge_Communication-Efficient_Robust_and_Fast_LiDAR_Map_Merging_Framework_for_Multi-Robot_Coordination_in_Resource-Constrained_Scenarios) (snippets)
- The collaborative-GS survey lists fusing data from heterogeneous robot teams as an open challenge — [arXiv 2510.23988](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23988v1) (snippet)
- CGS-SLAM's authors note that an earlier multi-agent GS SLAM relied on RGB-D input — [arXiv 2608.26868](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26868) (snippet)

### Inferences
- For the user's setting (RealSense and ZED with a measured 6–9% colour offset), the nearest usable tools are per-camera affine or bilateral-grid appearance models (Robust GS, MCSplat) and HAMMER-style per-device handling. None has been evaluated in a multi-session, multi-robot, LiDAR-posed GS map.
- Because LiDAR-inertial odometry already supplies poses, the photometric-residual pose-graph approach of MAGS-SLAM matters less than appearance modelling during map fusion. That is where Gaussians would otherwise "bake in" the colour offset (per the DAVIGS observation).

### Gaps
- I found no multi-agent GS paper that reports per-agent intrinsics or resolution differences explicitly (e.g. 640x480 RealSense vs 1280x720 ZED) or quantifies cross-agent colour offsets.
- No dataset was found that pairs different camera models across robots and sessions with GS evaluation.

## Communication: data volume of exchanged maps; compression and prioritisation of Gaussian map exchange; value-of-information and scheduling

### Takeaway
Bandwidth is now an explicit topic. Coko-SLAM (85–95% less data), CoMA-SLAM (distributed, 99.8% claimed vs centralized), GS-Share (incremental map deltas, 36% less) and a CVPR 2026 workshop paper on importance-driven, communication-aware Gaussian sharing all address it. However, none was found that formulates **value-of-information scheduling** of Gaussian map updates under a wireless link model, or that uses per-Gaussian uncertainty for transmission decisions.

### Cited Findings
- Coko-SLAM: up to 85–95% less transmitted data than the state of the art, and submaps sent in seconds rather than minutes (vs MAGiC-SLAM) — [arXiv 2604.00804](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00804) (snippet; absolute MB values not seen)
- CoMA-SLAM: "efficiently manages communication bandwidth while dynamically scaling with the number of agents", with a claimed 99.8% bandwidth reduction vs centralized approaches — [AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37283) (snippet)
- MAGiC-SLAM sends every finished submap to the server — [CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yugay_MAGiC-SLAM_Multi-Agent_Gaussian_Globally_Consistent__SLAM_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf) (snippet)
- MAGS-SLAM transmits "compact submap summaries" rather than raw observations or dense maps — [arXiv 2605.10760](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10760v1) (snippet)
- GS-Share (Eurographics 2025 / CGF 44(7), Zhang, Zhu, Duan, Zhang, USTC): incremental 3DGS map sharing. It sends the full map once and then only the map increments, cutting transmission overhead by 36% and improving extrapolated-view PSNR/LPIPS/Depth-L1 by about 11%/22%/74% vs SOTA on a Replica-based benchmark. A secondary summary says the baseline was HAC and that increments use a 50%-smaller feature dimension (unverified) — [arXiv 2510.02884](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02884); [Lacuna summary](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/gs-share-enabling-high-fidelity-map-sharing-with-incremental-gaussian-splatting/wrk_389d7af7d4671debdf704bf25f9faeb6) (snippet)
- "Importance-Driven 3D Gaussian SLAM for Efficient Mapping and Communication-Aware Sharing" (Yang et al., CVPR 2026 Workshop DriveX): ranks Gaussians by a multi-view importance score ("PWAS") and shares only the top ones. At equal bandwidth it reportedly beats random selection and brings partial-observation agents close to full-information baselines — [CVPRW 2026 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/DriveX/papers/Yang_Importance-Driven_3D_Gaussian_SLAM_for_Efficient_Mapping_and_Communication-Aware_Sharing_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf); [workshop PDF](https://drivex-workshop.github.io/cvpr2026/static/pdf/24_Importance_Driven_3D_Gaussi.pdf) (snippet only; a second, exact-title search did not find it again, so the details are unverified)
- GSCOOP (ICCV 2025): multi-vehicle collaborative semantic segmentation by sharing sparse 3D Gaussians. Its compression brings bandwidth to 62 Kbps–7 Mbps — [ICCV 2025 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Hong_Communication-Efficient_Multi-Vehicle_Collaborative_Semantic_Segmentation_via_Sparse_3D_Gaussian_Sharing_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) (snippet)
- VOGS-CP (AAAI 2026, Purdue): vision-only Gaussian splatting for collaborative occupancy prediction, using 34.6% of the communication volume — [Purdue PDF](https://engineering.purdue.edu/dcsl/publications/papers/2026/Vision-Only-Gaussian-Splatting-AAAI26.pdf) (snippet)
- STT-GS (edge GS): selects clients by predicted information gain, jointly with power control. This works per client or image, not per Gaussian — [arXiv 2510.13186](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13186) (snippet)
- RT-GuIDE: robots keep private 3DGS maps and never share splats, so message size does not grow with map size. Information gain is evaluated against a pooled map — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395870671_RT-GuIDE_Real-Time_Gaussian_Splatting_for_Information-Driven_Exploration) (snippet)
- GS-NFS: bandwidth-adaptive streaming of dynamic Gaussian splats and point clouds — [arXiv 2606.05650](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05650) (title only)
- A federated Gaussian mapping framework for post-disaster rescue robots reportedly sends 13.45 MB per round, with the model compressed to about 10% — [Bioengineer news summary](https://bioengineer.org/rescue-robots-learn-to-map-collapsing-ruins-in-4d-while-sharing-almost-nothing/) (secondary news source; original paper not identified)
- Classical context: Kimera-Multi is fully distributed and peer-to-peer and is communication-efficient — [arXiv 2106.14386](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386). Swarm-SLAM has adjustable communication and computation budgets and broadcasts only after a displacement threshold — [arXiv 2301.06230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06230). A 2025 overview argues for goal-oriented communication in multi-agent systems — [arXiv 2508.07720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07720). One snippet estimates dense approaches at about 150 MB per robot, enough to saturate WiFi with a few agents — [search snippet, source among the Kimera-Multi/Swarm-SLAM results](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.03136) (attribution uncertain)

### Inferences
- The communication work splits into (i) cutting what is sent (compact submaps, descriptors instead of images: Coko, MAGS), (ii) removing the server (CoMA), (iii) sending deltas (GS-Share) and (iv) ranking Gaussians by importance (CVPRW 2026). None was seen modelling a real wireless channel (throughput variation, outages) or deciding *when* to send based on the expected effect on the receiving robot's localisation or task.
- Reported bandwidth figures are relative (%), measured on room-scale Replica/Aria maps. Nothing was found at the gigabyte, building-scale map size of the user's setting.

### Gaps
- Absolute MB transmitted for MAGiC/CP/MAC-Ego3D/Coko (Coko Table 4) could not be read.
- No evaluation was found over a real Wi-Fi or mesh link for GS map exchange.

## Multi-session / lifelong Gaussian maps and change handling

### Takeaway
Single-robot change detection and update on 3DGS is active: 3DGS-CD, GaME, GaussianUpdate, LT-Gaussian, GS-LTS, SI-Update, plus 3DGS-registration methods for merging maps (Graph-GSReg, SIREN, RegGS). None found combines **two robots with different cameras, LiDAR-posed multi-session merging and change detection at building scale on real robot data**.

### Cited Findings
- 3DGS-CD (Lu, Ye, Leonard; arXiv 2411.03706): the first 3DGS-based object-rearrangement change detection. It trains on pre-change images, localises post-change cameras, renders views, finds 2D changes with EfficientSAM and fuses them into 3D change masks and object transforms. It works with as few as one post-change image, is up to 14% more accurate and about 1000x faster than the previous radiance-field CD method, and is tested on public and self-collected data — [arXiv 2411.03706](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03706v2) (snippet)
- GaME, "Gaussian Mapping for Evolving Scenes": the first NVS-capable mapping system for long-term evolving scenes, using Dynamic Scene Adaptation. It criticises prior work for assuming each session is static and offline — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392530359_Gaussian_Mapping_for_Evolving_Scenes) (snippet)
- GaussianUpdate (ICCV 2025): continual 3DGS update in three stages — global appearance update for lighting via a 4D hash grid plus tiny MLP, then a geometric update, then joint refinement. Stated limitation: strong reflections (mirrors) — [ICCV 2025 PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zeng_GaussianUpdate_Continual_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_Update_for_Changing_Environments_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) (snippet)
- LT-Gaussian (arXiv 2508.01704): multimodal (camera+LiDAR) GS for driving. It compares the outdated Gaussian map with the current LiDAR stream to detect appearing and disappearing structure, then updates only the changed areas — [arXiv 2508.01704](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01704) (snippet)
- GS-LTS (arXiv 2503.17733): 3DGS for long-term service robots. It detects changes from a single image, collects multi-view images around the change and edits the Gaussians — [arXiv 2503.17733](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17733) (snippet)
- SI-Update: updates a 3DGS map from a single post-change RGB image for indoor object rearrangement — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6958781) (snippet)
- Lifelong 3D Mapping Framework (arXiv 2501.18110): LiDAR-only, with multi-session alignment, change detection and map version control — [arXiv 2501.18110](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.18110) (snippet)
- OASIS-Map (arXiv 2607.14899): object-level change detection in multi-session mapping using semantic correspondences (not stated to be GS) — [arXiv 2607.14899](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14899) (snippet)
- Merging 3DGS maps: Graph-GSReg (ECCV 2026) does scene-graph matching for GS registration plus merge refinement to reduce holes and floaters — [arXiv 2606.29782](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29782); [ECCV poster](https://eccv.ecva.net/virtual/2026/poster/5375). SIREN registers multi-robot GS maps — [arXiv 2502.06519](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06519). A skeleton-alignment 3DGS fusion method reports 41.9% lower RRE — [arXiv 2507.20480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20480). RegGS (ICCV 2025) does Sim(3) sub-map registration — [ICCV PDF](https://www.openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Cheng_RegGS_Unposed_Sparse_Views_Gaussian_Splatting_with_3DGS_Registration_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf) (snippets)
- DGOMapping uses a multi-agent "growth–forgetting–cleanup" lifecycle for 4D GS maps — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13306729/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- The change-detection methods assume a single camera model across sessions. Under a 6–9% inter-camera colour offset, a render-and-compare detector (3DGS-CD, GS-LTS) would likely flag the colour offset itself as change unless the appearance is harmonised first. This is my inference and is not tested in the sources. GaussianUpdate's global appearance stage is the closest mechanism.
- LT-Gaussian's LiDAR-vs-Gaussian geometric change test fits robots that carry LiDAR, because it avoids depending on colour.

### Gaps
- Scale and real-robot status of GaME and LT-Gaussian were not confirmed (LT-Gaussian targets driving).
- No building-scale multi-session GS benchmark with two robot platforms was found.

## Verified research gaps (each checked against counter-evidence)

### Takeaway
(a) Heterogeneous-camera plus LiDAR-inertial multi-agent GS mapping is **open**: HAMMER is the closest and only partly closes it. (b) Bandwidth-aware sharing is **partly closed** (Coko-SLAM, CoMA-SLAM, GS-Share, the CVPRW 2026 importance-driven work), but value-of-information *scheduling* over a real wireless link is **open**. (c) Multi-session merging with change detection on real robot data at building scale is **open** for the multi-robot, heterogeneous-camera case. Single-robot pieces exist (3DGS-CD, GaussianUpdate, LT-Gaussian, Graph-GSReg).

### Cited Findings
**Gap (a): multi-agent GS mapping with heterogeneous camera models and colour responses, and LiDAR-inertial agents — status: OPEN (partly addressed in pieces)**
- Counter-evidence searched: "collaborative Gaussian splatting heterogeneous cameras / intrinsics / mixed LiDAR", "multi-robot LiDAR camera Gaussian splatting", "multi-robot LiDAR-inertial Gaussian map merging". No system combining these was found — [search summaries citing Coko/MAGS/CGS](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00804)
- Closest work: **HAMMER** (heterogeneous devices, different image sensors and SLAM stacks, pre-processing adjustment, server-based, RGB+depth, no LiDAR) — [arXiv 2501.14147](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14147v1)
- Second closest: **MAGS-SLAM** (photometric residuals, but a homogeneous monocular team) — [arXiv 2605.10760](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10760v1)
- Building blocks: MCSplat / MC-Gaussian (cross-camera appearance in driving rigs) — [arXiv 2610.25345](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25345); RMGS-SLAM / Gaussian-LIC2 (single-robot LiDAR-inertial-visual GS) — [arXiv 2604.12942](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12942v1)
- The survey names heterogeneous-source fusion as an open challenge — [arXiv 2510.23988](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23988v1)

**Gap (b): bandwidth-aware or value-driven sharing of Gaussian map content between robots — status: PARTLY CLOSED**
- Closed parts: compact submaps (Coko-SLAM, 85–95% less) — [arXiv 2604.00804](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00804); distributed exchange (CoMA-SLAM) — [AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37283); incremental deltas (GS-Share, 36% less) — [arXiv 2510.02884](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02884); importance-ranked Gaussian sharing at a fixed bandwidth (**closest work:** "Importance-Driven 3D Gaussian SLAM ... Communication-Aware Sharing", CVPRW 2026, snippet only) — [CVPRW PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/DriveX/papers/Yang_Importance-Driven_3D_Gaussian_SLAM_for_Efficient_Mapping_and_Communication-Aware_Sharing_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf); sparse Gaussian sharing for perception tasks (GSCOOP, VOGS-CP) — [ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Hong_Communication-Efficient_Multi-Vehicle_Collaborative_Semantic_Segmentation_via_Sparse_3D_Gaussian_Sharing_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)
- Still open: (i) VoI or uncertainty-driven *per-Gaussian* selection, where Fisher-information uncertainty exists (POp-GS, ActiveSplat) but is used only for view selection, not transmission — [POp-GS CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Wilson_POp-GS_Next_Best_View_in_3D-Gaussian_Splatting_with_P-Optimality_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf); (ii) scheduling under a measured wireless channel; (iii) GB-scale building maps. STT-GS does information-gain selection only per client — [arXiv 2510.13186](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13186)

**Gap (c): multi-session merging with change detection on real robot data at building scale — status: OPEN for multi-robot / heterogeneous cameras; PARTLY CLOSED single-robot**
- Closest works: **GaME** (multi-session evolving-scene GS) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392530359_Gaussian_Mapping_for_Evolving_Scenes); **3DGS-CD** (object-level, small scenes) — [arXiv 2411.03706](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03706v2); **LT-Gaussian** (LiDAR-based GS change detection and update, driving) — [arXiv 2508.01704](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01704); **GaussianUpdate** (appearance and geometry continual update) — [ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zeng_GaussianUpdate_Continual_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_Update_for_Changing_Environments_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf); **Graph-GSReg / SIREN** (merging GS maps) — [arXiv 2606.29782](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29782)
- The search "Gaussian splatting long-term map update real robot multi-session building-scale" returned no paper combining real robot, building-scale, multi-session and appearance change — [search summary listing LT-Gaussian, GaME, GaussianUpdate, SI-Update, DGOMapping](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zeng_GaussianUpdate_Continual_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_Update_for_Changing_Environments_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The strongest combined gap for the user's platform is **cross-camera appearance harmonisation as a precondition for cross-session change detection and fusion in a LiDAR-posed multi-robot GS map**. Each ingredient exists on its own (MCSplat-style canonical appearance, LT-Gaussian-style LiDAR change test, Graph-GSReg merging), but no paper was found combining them. The 6–9% measured colour offset is exactly the error that render-and-compare change detectors would misread as change.
- A second publishable angle is **value-driven delta sharing**: combine GS-Share-style increments with importance or Fisher-uncertainty ranking, under a bandwidth budget measured on a real robot-to-robot Wi-Fi link, at GB-scale maps. The CVPRW 2026 importance-driven paper is the work most likely to close this, and it should be read in full before claiming novelty.
- A low-risk extra gap: no multi-agent GS SLAM uses LiDAR-inertial odometry as the pose source and treats the GS map purely as a shared appearance and geometry layer. Existing systems couple GS tracking to the map. The closest designs are single-robot LIV GS SLAM (RMGS-SLAM, Gaussian-LIC2) and server-based HAMMER, which accepts arbitrary on-board SLAM poses.

### Gaps
- Full texts of the closest papers (HAMMER, the CVPRW 2026 importance-driven paper, GaME, Coko-SLAM, MAGS-SLAM) need to be read to confirm that they do not already handle per-agent colour response or VoI scheduling. That was not possible with arxiv.org blocked, and the CVPRW paper was found by only one of two searches.
- Not checked: ICRA/IROS 2026 proceedings directly, and RSS 2026. A 2026 paper closing gap (a) or (c) may exist without having surfaced in web search.
