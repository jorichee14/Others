# Let LiDAR-inertial odometry explain the pixels

The most important open direction for your two-robot setup is a **sensor-driven physical image-formation model**. It uses the continuous-time LiDAR-inertial trajectory to supply the camera poses inside each exposure (for motion blur, and for row timing if the cameras are rolling shutter). Per-frame exposure drives both brightness and blur length, and a per-camera ISP absorbs the RealSense/ZED colour difference. The model is built for LiDAR-anchored NeST surfels and tested on views held out from the other robot. Every ingredient exists somewhere: VIO-driven blur and rolling shutter on smartphones, visual-inertial rolling-shutter timing on Aria glasses, exposure-coupled blur in handheld HDR video, a physically based ISP with a novel-view controller, and an online robot deblurrer that integrates over the exposure. **No paper found combines them with a LiDAR-inertial trajectory, a ground robot, building scale, heterogeneous cameras and a surfel representation.** Generative completion of unobserved regions is a crowded field: diffusion "fixers" and geometry-conditioned video priors already work at room scale. What stays open there is narrower and more defensible: **generation constrained by metric LiDAR, with a persistent measured-versus-generated label, validated against real captures it never saw.** Multi-agent Gaussian SLAM is almost entirely RGB-D, room-scale and limited to one camera type. Teams that mix LiDAR-inertial robots with different cameras are open, while bandwidth-aware sharing is partly closed. **All verdicts below were checked only against search snippets**, because arxiv.org and several aggregators were unreachable in the research sessions, so each "open" label is provisional until the re-check list at the end has been done. The strongest first paper is the image-formation model plus cross-camera appearance, evaluated on held-out views from the other robot, with "does it stop surfel drift?" as its central question.

## Five measurements on your data rank the directions

Your own measurements do most of the ranking. Four of the five symptoms are image-formation or appearance errors that the robot's own sensors could explain. Only the ceiling deficit calls for a generative prior. None of them calls for better communication between two robots that already have LiDAR-inertial poses.

| Measured on your data | Direction it implicates | Literature maturity (snippet-level) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blur follows turning, ~12% of frames | Sensor-driven blur model (rotation rates come straight from the IMU) | Learned-trajectory deblurring is mature; LiDAR-inertial-driven deblurring was not found | High |
| Exposure varies ±13% across frames | Exposure/ISP model coupled to blur length | Learned per-view correction is mature; metadata-driven coupling is partly closed | High |
| 6–9% colour offset between the two cameras | Per-camera ISP / cross-agent harmonisation | Solved for single-rig driving cameras; not carried into multi-robot GS | High (folds into the first paper) |
| A quarter of surfels drift >10 cm off the LiDAR map, floaters at glass | Geometry absorbing unmodelled photometric error, plus LiDAR anchoring | Indirect evidence only; no surfel deblurring work found | High (the test that makes the first paper) |
| ~20% of surfaces barely observed, mostly ceiling | Generative completion with provenance | Fixers are mature; LiDAR-constrained provenance is open | Medium (second paper) |

The drift row connects the others, and it suggests a hypothesis the literature hints at but has not tested. Several papers report that when appearance variation goes unmodelled, the primitives absorb it. DAVIGS notes that without a per-image appearance model, Gaussians "tend to adjust their colors" to fit appearance changes ([arXiv 2501.10788](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10788)). MCSplat builds a canonical appearance space precisely so that cross-camera colour differences do not leak into geometry ([arXiv 2610.25345](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25345)). Bilateral-grid ISP modelling reduces floaters by absorbing exposure and tone effects ([arXiv 2406.00448](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00448v2)). DeblurGS anneals densification because Gaussians spawn in the wrong places while the blur trajectory is still wrong ([arXiv 2404.11358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11358v2)). If blur, exposure and colour offset are pulling your trainable surfel positions off the LiDAR surface, a correct image-formation model should pull them back. That result would be measurable, novel, and would matter for downstream geometry. The causal link on your data is an **untested inference**, and it is the first experiment to run. The glass floaters are a partly separate problem: the notes did not survey transparency or reflection handling. The only related evidence is GaussianUpdate listing mirrors as a stated limitation ([ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zeng_GaussianUpdate_Continual_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_Update_for_Changing_Environments_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)). Expect glass to stay a residual failure case rather than something image formation fixes.

## Image formation: every piece exists, but never driven by LiDAR-inertial odometry

### Learned-trajectory deblurring is mature, expensive, and blind to sensors

From 2024 to 2026, 3DGS deblurring meant learning the camera's motion inside each exposure from images alone. BAD-Gaussians (ECCV 2024) fits a spline trajectory per blurry image, renders **10 virtual sharp poses** and averages them to reproduce the blur. It still depends on COLMAP initialisation, which its authors admit is hard under blur ([arXiv 2403.11831](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.11831)). In its supplement, linear interpolation beat cubic B-splines on synthetic data, but splines won on real data with longer exposures ([ECCV supp](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/03633-supp.pdf)). Deblur-GS offers linear, spline and Bezier trajectories ([GitHub](https://github.com/Chaphlagical/Deblur-GS)). DeblurGS adds sub-frame alignment and densification annealing and claims to recover from noisy SfM poses ([arXiv 2404.11358](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11358v2)). Lee et al. (ECCV 2024) skip the trajectory: a training-only MLP predicts offset Gaussian sets that are averaged, so inference costs nothing extra ([arXiv 2401.00834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.00834)). BAGS predicts per-pixel blur kernels without any camera-motion model ([arXiv 2403.04926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04926)). Newer variants push trajectory capacity further: CRiM-GS uses neural ODEs ([arXiv 2407.03923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03923v2)) and BSGS uses **21 sub-poses** ([arXiv 2510.12493](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12493)). Training cost grows roughly linearly with the number of sub-frames. The one escape seen is RoMo-GS (arXiv 2610.24605, October 2026). It integrates each Gaussian's contribution analytically over the exposure in an online robot mapper and reports roughly **15–150x speed-ups** over offline deblurring on handheld, legged and arm platforms. That claim is **secondary-source only** and camera-only as far as the summary says ([radiancefields](https://radiancefields.com/papers/romo-gs-efficient-online-3d-gaussian-splatting-for-robot-mapping-via-exposure-integrated-motion-blur)). Across this whole family the intra-exposure trajectory is a free variable fit to a photometric loss, and that ambiguity is exactly what a gyroscope removes.

### Sensor-informed methods exist only for phones, glasses, thermal rigs and cars

A smaller line injects sensor data into image formation. Gaussian Splatting on the Move (ECCV 2024) initialises linear and angular velocities from smartphone VIO and models blur and rolling shutter in screen space ([arXiv 2403.13327](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.13327v3)). Its repository states that joint blur and rolling-shutter compensation needs **known exposure and readout times** ([GitHub](https://github.com/spectacularai/3dgs-deblur)). It was evaluated on 11 short phone scenes ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10848124)). Lv et al. (SIGGRAPH 2025, Meta) use visual-inertial bundle adjustment to calibrate rolling-shutter timing on Aria glasses. They add rolling shutter, lens shading, exposure and gain to the splatting model and report about **+1 dB from the trajectory and another +1 dB from the image-formation model**. The snippets did not mention motion blur ([arXiv 2506.04444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04444v1)). TRGS-SLAM (March 2026) combines an IMU loss with B-spline trajectories for blurry, rolling-shutter thermal images on robots ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20443v1)). The KAIST ICCV 2025 extreme-blur work is the closest depth-sensor analogue. It aligns RGB-D frames by optical flow and ICP, models exposure motion by start and end poses, and initialises dense Gaussians from depth instead of SfM ([ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Jang_Splat-based_3D_Scene_Reconstruction_with_Extreme_Motion-blur_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)). In driving, SplatAD and NeuRAD use per-ray timestamps for camera and LiDAR rolling shutter. NeuRAD's ablations show that rolling shutter is essential for sensor realism ([SplatAD CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Hess_SplatAD_Real-Time_Lidar_and_Camera_Rendering_with_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_CVPR_2025_paper.html); [NeuRAD CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Tonderski_NeuRAD_Neural_Rendering_for_Autonomous_Driving_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)). 3DGUT handles rolling shutter by transforming each sigma point with its own capture-time pose and notes that EWA splatting represents rolling shutter poorly ([arXiv 2412.12507](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.12507v2)). A 2026 follow-up gives every pixel its own capture time ([arXiv 2602.05617](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05617)).

LiDAR-inertial-visual Gaussian SLAM, the family your stack belongs to, uses the sensors for pose and geometry, not image formation. Gaussian-LIC models varying camera exposure outdoors ([arXiv 2404.06926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06926)). Gaussian-LIC2 has a continuous-time LiDAR-inertial-camera trajectory, but the snippets showed no blur or rolling-shutter model ([arXiv 2507.04004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04004v1)). VIGS-SLAM is motivated by blur but uses the IMU only for tracking robustness ([arXiv 2512.02293](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02293v1)). FAST-LIVO2 estimates inverse exposure time in its filter state, but for odometry, not rendering ([arXiv 2408.14035](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14035v2)). The sensor that already gives you a high-rate continuous trajectory has never been wired into a splatting blur model in anything the search found.

### Exposure and ISP modelling is converging on physical models with controllers

Most systems absorb auto-exposure with per-view learned corrections, and these do not transfer to novel views. Bilateral Guided Radiance Field Processing fits per-view 3D bilateral grids and tells users to disable them for test views ([arXiv 2406.00448](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00448v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/yuehaowang/bilarf)). Luminance-GS (CVPR 2025) learns per-view colour matrices and tone curves ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Cui_Luminance-GS_Adapting_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_to_Challenging_Lighting_Conditions_with_CVPR_2025_paper.html)). IBGS observes that per-view affine transforms cannot generalise to novel views ([arXiv 2511.14357](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14357)). Metadata-aware methods are a minority. HDR-GS conditions its tone mapper on log exposure time but needs multi-exposure inputs with known times ([arXiv 2405.15125](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15125v4)). Casual3DHDR (ACM MM 2025) jointly estimates exposure time, camera response and a continuous trajectory from auto-exposure video. It is the only method found that **ties blur extent and brightness to the same exposure variable** ([arXiv 2504.17728](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17728v2)). NVIDIA's PPISP (January 2026) separates camera-intrinsic effects (vignetting, response curve) from per-frame settings (exposure, white balance) and adds a controller that predicts ISP parameters for novel views. Removing its exposure term drops novel-view PSNR from **24.62 to 23.33 dB** ([arXiv 2601.18336](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18336)). AERGS-SLAM (CVPR 2026) brings a learned camera-response exposure network into stereo GS SLAM ([CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Zhou_AERGS-SLAM_Auto-Exposure-Robust_Stereo_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_SLAM_CVPR_2026_paper.html)). For cross-camera colour, the tools come from single-rig driving: MCSplat's canonical appearance space ([arXiv 2610.25345](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25345)), MC-Gaussian's pose-conditioned correction ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-22264-0_30)), Robust GS's per-camera affine transform ([arXiv 2404.04211](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04211)) and NeuRAD's per-sensor embeddings ([CVPR 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Tonderski_NeuRAD_Neural_Rendering_for_Autonomous_Driving_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)). No paper was found that feeds RealSense or ZED per-frame exposure metadata into NeRF or GS.

### Nobody measures sharpness away from the training trajectory

Deblurring benchmarks are object- or room-scale and score sharp references captured close to the blurry training views. The Deblur-NeRF real set takes its references with a still camera near the training poses ([CVPR 2022 supp](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/supplemental/Ma_Deblur-NeRF_Neural_Radiance_CVPR_2022_supplemental.pdf)). ExBluRF adds ground-truth blur trajectories but stays small ([arXiv 2309.08957](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.08957v3)). The one large robot benchmark with deliberately off-trajectory test views, Oxford Spires, found that Nerfacto and Splatfacto **overfit to training poses and generalise poorly out of sequence**. It uses global-shutter cameras and does not stress blur, rolling shutter or auto-exposure ([arXiv 2411.10546](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10546v2)). On robot platforms the effect is large even for classical SLAM: a 2026 quadruped study saw rolling-shutter cameras drop to **33 feature matches against 188** for global shutter under motion blur ([arXiv 2606.19067](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19067)). Two robots driving different paths through the same building give you the held-out, off-trajectory views this literature lacks.

The biggest threat to novelty is erosion from three directions. ## Generative completion: fixers work, provenance does not exist yet

### Diffusion fixers repair renders but know nothing about metric geometry

The dominant recipe is: render a novel view, clean it with a fine-tuned diffusion model, and distil it back as a pseudo-target while moving progressively away from the training cameras. Difix3D+ (NVIDIA, CVPR 2025 oral) does this with a **single-step** Stable-Diffusion-derived model. It reports about **2x better FID** and also works as a real-time post-render enhancer ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Wu_DIFIX3D_Improving_3D_Reconstructions_with_Single-Step_Diffusion_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.html)). Its model card warns that it is "not guaranteed to fix 100% of the image artifacts", restricts use under the NVIDIA License, and points to a commercial successor called "Fixer" ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/difix)). GSFix3D trains with random masks so it can inpaint holes and is the only fixer evaluated indoors, on ScanNet++ and Replica ([arXiv 2508.14717](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14717)). GSFixer (ICML 2026) conditions a CogVideoX video DiT on DINOv2 and VGGT features from reference views ([arXiv 2508.09667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.09667)). 3DGS-Enhancer (NeurIPS 2024) recasts restoration as temporally consistent video generation ([arXiv 2410.16266](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16266v1)). ConFixGS (May 2026) adds a step that matters for your goal: it cross-checks diffusion pseudo-targets against support views by reprojection and weights refinement by the resulting confidence, gaining up to **+3.68 dB** on driving data ([arXiv 2605.09688](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09688)). No snippet showed any fixer using metric depth or LiDAR as a hard constraint during distillation, so a fixed appearance can drag geometry wherever it is photometrically convenient. That failure mode matches your drift problem.

### Geometry-conditioned video priors stop at room scale

Generative priors moved from per-scene distillation (ReconFusion, about an hour per scene) to multi-view diffusion (CAT3D, minutes) ([arXiv 2405.10314](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10314)), then to video diffusion conditioned on a rendered 3D structure. ViewCrafter conditions on DUSt3R point clouds. Other authors report that it "generates many unreasonable contents" when input overlap is small and degrades when DUSt3R mispredicts planar geometry ([ReconX](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.16767v4)). ReconX encodes a global point cloud as the condition and reports PSNR 24.57 against 18.83 for MVSplat ([arXiv 2408.16767](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.16767v1)). GEN3C keeps a persistent "3D cache" of unprojected depth, renders it along the camera path and ships as the 7B-parameter Cosmos model aimed at data-centre GPUs ([project](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/GEN3C); [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/GEN3C-Cosmos-7B)). G4Splat (ICLR 2026) makes the decisive argument for your ceiling problem. Indoor planar structure gives scale-accurate geometry that improves visibility masks and multi-view consistency during inpainting, and its ablation shows that **the generative prior alone gives limited geometry gains and blurry unseen areas** ([arXiv 2510.12099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12099)). Indoor completion and generation systems such as GenRC, MVRoom, Rein3D and L3DG are all room- or panorama-scale ([MVRoom](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04248v1); [Rein3D](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.10578)). ViewExtrapolator argues that extrapolation should be scored with LPIPS rather than PSNR because the task is "more generative than regressive" ([arXiv 2411.14208](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14208)). That is a warning about how any ceiling result will be judged. A low forward-facing camera turns the ceiling into extreme upward extrapolation, the regime where point-cloud-conditioned video models hallucinate most. Metric LiDAR on the ceiling, where your scanner's vertical field of view reaches it, replaces exactly the DUSt3R or monocular geometry these methods fail on.

### Commercial world models blend capture and generation without saying where

SpAItial Echo-3, launched on 27 October 2026, turns iPhone walkthroughs into metric-scale 3D worlds by combining "the capture with what it has learned about how real spaces look". It runs as a cloud service, its benchmarks are vendor-reported, and there is no technical report ([Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/spaitial-echo-3-turns-iphone-scans-into-3d-worlds); [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/spaitial-echo-3-iphone-3d-world-capture)). World Labs Marble exports about **2M splats** and is reported to work better for interiors ([Scenario help](https://help.scenario.com/articles/4774055514-marble-by-world-labs-the-essentials)). That splat budget points to room or apartment scale, not 6000 m², though this is an inference. Feed-forward reconstructors such as AnySplat interpolate within their input views rather than completing unseen regions ([arXiv 2505.23716](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23716v2); [AnchorSplat](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.07053.md)). The notes found nothing showing whether Echo-3 or Marble mark captured versus generated regions. That opacity is itself an argument for publishing the provenance problem.

### Uncertainty and hallucination masks exist, but only as training-time weights

Two lines are converging without having met. Classical radiance-field uncertainty identifies under-constrained regions: FisherRF computes Fisher information cheaply enough for view selection at 70 fps ([arXiv 2311.17874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17874v1)), and PH-Dropout notes that GS lacks systematic epistemic uncertainty quantification ([arXiv 2410.05468](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05468v1)). Hallucination-aware generative reconstruction emerged in 2026. HAD (CVPR 2026) learns a per-pixel hallucination score and excludes masked pixels from the loss ([CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Liu_HAD_Hallucination-Aware_Diffusion_Priors_for_3D_Reconstruction_CVPR_2026_paper.html)). OracleGS (WACV 2026) validates generated views against multi-view-stereo attention ([MCML](https://mcml.ai/publications/tln+26/)). GA-GS learns a **per-primitive** reliability weight for generated content, the closest thing to a stored provenance tag ([arXiv 2604.04331](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04331v1)). LiDAR enters only separately. GeomGS scores each Gaussian by its distance to the nearest LiDAR point, with no generation involved ([arXiv 2501.13417](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13417v1)). LiDAR-GS++ generates LiDAR scans for under-fitted outdoor regions ([arXiv 2511.12304](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.12304)). Downstream, RFDT-Channel builds a 3DGS-to-mesh, LiDAR-refined model of an indoor space for 28 GHz ray tracing in Sionna, with no generative step ([Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.08035)). Real-to-sim robotics validates fidelity through correlation with real policy success ([arXiv 2511.04665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04665)). Nobody has measured what a hallucinated ceiling does to either.

## Collaborative Gaussian SLAM assumes one camera and no LiDAR

### Systems grew from two agents to distributed, but stayed RGB-D and room-scale

CP-SLAM (NeurIPS 2023) introduced collaborative neural-point SLAM for two agents. Its authors flag heavy GPU needs and drift during map fusion under large viewpoint changes ([NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7c10e259c7e56fa218ee03d9ae7d728e-Paper-Conference.pdf)). MAGiC-SLAM (CVPR 2025) centralises finished Gaussian submaps on a server for loop closure and fusion ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Yugay_MAGiC-SLAM_Multi-Agent_Gaussian_Globally_Consistent__SLAM_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)). A competitor reports that it fails outdoors on Kimera-Multi ([arXiv 2506.18885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18885)). MAC-Ego3D (CVPR 2025) uses asynchronous Gaussian consensus and reports **40.04 dB against 29.33 dB** for CP-SLAM on Multiagent Replica ([arXiv 2412.09723](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09723v1)). GRAND-SLAM takes RGB-D GS SLAM outdoors, reporting 91% lower multi-agent tracking error on Kimera-Multi ([arXiv 2506.18885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18885)). CoMA-SLAM (AAAI 2026) is billed as the first distributed multi-agent Gaussian SLAM. It uses 2D surfels, the closest representation to NeST, and claims a 99.8% bandwidth reduction against centralised designs ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37283)). The 2026 wave relaxes sensors in the other direction. MAGS-SLAM is monocular with photometric residuals in a Sim(3) pose graph ([arXiv 2605.10760](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10760v1)), and CGS-SLAM runs on smartphone RGB and IMU with Depth Pro for scale ([HF](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.26868)). HAMMER (RA-L 2025) alone targets heterogeneous devices. It aligns robots and Aria glasses running different on-board SLAM stacks and "adjusts automatically for variations in image pre-processing" ([arXiv 2501.14147](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.14147v1)). The snippets did not show whether that adjustment is a colour model. **None of these systems ingests LiDAR**, and the standard benchmarks use one camera model per sequence set. The collaborative-GS survey names "fusing data from heterogeneous sources" as a core open challenge ([arXiv 2510.23988](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23988v1)). LiDAR-inertial-visual GS SLAM exists only for single robots: RMGS-SLAM ([arXiv 2604.12942](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12942v1)), Gaussian-LIC2 and LVI-GS ([arXiv 2411.02703](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.02703)). Multi-robot LiDAR merging exists only without Gaussians, for example LEMON-Mapping ([arXiv 2505.10018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.10018)).

### Bandwidth work reports relative savings on small maps

Communication is now a named topic, but every figure is a percentage measured on Replica or Aria maps. Coko-SLAM sends only keyframe descriptors, poses and pure Gaussian submaps and claims **85–95% less data** than prior methods ([arXiv 2604.00804](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.00804)). GS-Share sends map increments after one full transfer and reports **36% less overhead** ([arXiv 2510.02884](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02884)). A CVPR 2026 workshop paper ranks Gaussians by a multi-view importance score and shares only the top ones at a fixed bandwidth ([CVPRW 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/DriveX/papers/Yang_Importance-Driven_3D_Gaussian_SLAM_for_Efficient_Mapping_and_Communication-Aware_Sharing_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf)). It turned up in only one of two searches. For perception tasks, GSCOOP shares sparse Gaussians at **62 Kbps–7 Mbps** ([ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Hong_Communication-Efficient_Multi-Vehicle_Collaborative_Semantic_Segmentation_via_Sparse_3D_Gaussian_Sharing_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)). STT-GS selects by information gain per client, not per Gaussian ([arXiv 2510.13186](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13186)). Fisher-information uncertainty is used for view selection (POp-GS) but never for deciding what to transmit ([CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Wilson_POp-GS_Next_Best_View_in_3D-Gaussian_Splatting_with_P-Optimality_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf)). No evaluation over a real Wi-Fi or mesh link was found.

### Multi-session change detection would misread your colour offset as change

Single-robot change handling on Gaussian maps is active. 3DGS-CD detects object rearrangement from as little as one post-change image by rendering and comparing ([arXiv 2411.03706](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03706v2)). GaussianUpdate updates appearance, then geometry, continually ([ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zeng_GaussianUpdate_Continual_3D_Gaussian_Splatting_Update_for_Changing_Environments_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)). LT-Gaussian compares an old Gaussian map with the live LiDAR stream to find structural change in driving ([arXiv 2508.01704](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01704)). Graph-GSReg (ECCV 2026) registers and merges GS maps while reducing holes and floaters ([arXiv 2606.29782](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29782)). All of them assume one camera model across sessions. A render-and-compare detector fed a ZED view of a RealSense-built map would likely flag your **6–9% colour offset** as change. That is an inference, not tested in any source. LT-Gaussian's geometric test avoids colour entirely, which suits LiDAR-carrying robots.

## The strongest first paper couples LiDAR-inertial image formation with cross-camera appearance

The strongest first paper combines gaps **1a, 1b, 1c and 1d with 1f**, evaluated using **1g** on your own data. Drive surfel rendering with the continuous-time GLIM trajectory: intra-exposure poses for blur, which matters most because your blur follows turning and gyro rates capture it directly, and per-row poses only if the datasheets confirm rolling shutter. Feed per-frame exposure from driver metadata into both blur length and brightness, refined where it is noisy. Use a PPISP-style split of per-camera vignetting and response from per-frame settings, so that the RealSense and the ZED share one map. Anchor surfels to the LiDAR map with a GeomGS-style distance confidence. Evaluate on held-out frames from the *other* robot's trajectory, which gives the off-trajectory test that Oxford Spires showed matters and that deblurring benchmarks lack. Report geometry as well as photometry: the fraction of surfels more than 10 cm from LiDAR, floater counts, and PSNR/LPIPS on sharp held-out frames. The key ablations are LiDAR-inertial trajectory versus a learned spline (BAD-Gaussians-style) and versus a VIO-style zero-initialised velocity, metadata-driven exposure versus per-view bilateral grids, and per-camera ISP on and off. If modelling image formation measurably pulls your drifted surfels back onto the LiDAR surface, that becomes the headline result. If it does not, LiDAR anchoring carries the geometry claim and image formation carries the photometric one. The paper survives either outcome.

This combination wins on four counts. It addresses four of your five measured symptoms with data you already hold. Its novelty rests on a sensor, a platform, a representation and a geometry metric that none of the closest works share. It needs far less compute than video-diffusion pipelines. And it produces the "what is measured well" signal that a generative second paper needs: a LiDAR-constrained ceiling fill with persistent provenance, validated by an upward-looking pass from the second robot (gaps 2a and 2c). Multi-robot fusion with change detection (gaps 3a and 3c) is a natural third paper once appearance is harmonised. Leading with generation is riskier: the field moved fastest in 2026, and fixers trained on blurry, miscoloured renders inherit those errors.

## Conclusion

The three directions are less separate than the question implies. They share one underlying ledger of what the sensors actually measured. Image formation decides which pixels count as measurements rather than blur, exposure or ISP artefacts. Provenance in generative completion is only meaningful once that boundary is clean. Cross-robot fusion and change detection fail first on exactly the cross-camera appearance error that the image-formation model absorbs. A group that builds the sensor-driven measurement model first owns the foundation that the generative and collaborative papers sit on, and that ordering, more than any single gap, is the strategic advantage of starting with image formation.

The main uncertainty is not technical but bibliographic. Several of the closest papers (RoMo-GS, MCSplat, Echo-3) date from October 2026 and are known only from summaries. A fast-moving field and snippet-only verification mean the novelty window for gap 1a is measured in months, so the RoMo-GS and Lv et al. full-text checks should happen before any experiment is designed around them. Glass floaters are outside everything surveyed here and will likely remain a stated limitation rather than a contribution.
