# Lead SCooP-Bed with a validated, replayable link

SCooP-Bed's single highlight should be this: **it is a replayable measured link, and the replay is proven against live runs.** Every inter-agent Wi-Fi link is logged on the sensors' clock and in the sensors' map frame, and replaying that log into collaborative perception reproduces what the live system did. The highlight should not be heterogeneity, ground truth without motion capture, or "real links hurt". Heterogeneity is CoPeD's claim. Ground truth without mocap has precedent in CoPeD and Kimera-Multi. And CooperScene (2026) already argues that real C-V2X links break the "infeasible communication assumptions" of collaborative-perception benchmarks ([CooperScene](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31219)). What no robotics, V2X or ISAC dataset in the notes reports is the check every trace-replay system since Noble (1997) has used to earn trust: run the same application live and under replay, and report the error ([Noble et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/263105.263140); [Pantheon](https://pantheon.stanford.edu/faq)). Three supporting claims back the highlight, and the authors already hold numbers for each. First, the instrument is trustworthy in time and space: measured clock offset ≤0.99 ms, and 3.1 cm median ground truth with no mocap or GNSS. Second, the map predicts the radio: Fresnel clearance gives Spearman ρ = 0.60 and a 5.4 dB clear-vs-obstructed gap. Third, synthetic channels overstate the link: they deliver 100% of messages in-frame, against 86% measured with one robot and 59–68% with two. Planned experiment E3 (Twin Run) carries the whole highlight. Without a live-vs-replay error number, the paper has a strong dataset but not a proven instrument.

## Testbed papers name one enabling property, then prove it

Across about 30 precedents in four literatures, successful testbed papers do two things. They name a single property that makes the instrument usable, and they back it with the kind of evidence their community trusts. Each literature uses a different kind of evidence. That variety is why SCooP-Bed has to choose its frame on purpose.

**Robotics platforms prove value through adoption or through dataset completeness, not through measured fidelity.** The Robotarium's headline is access. Its enabling guarantee is provable collision avoidance, enforced at more than 30 Hz with control barrier functions, so strangers can run arbitrary code safely. It proves this with demonstrations and usage counts, such as "more than 250 groups from every continent but Antarctica" ([arXiv 1604.00640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00640); [THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/Articles/2017/08/17/New-Facility-Enables-Remote-Users-to-Control-Robot-Swarms.aspx)). Duckietown, AI-DO and F1TENTH make the same move with low cost, competitions and course enrolment. RA-L multi-robot dataset papers such as CoPeD, S3E and Kimera-Multi follow a different template: a comparison table against prior datasets, an explicit ground-truth method, and baseline benchmarks ([CoPeD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14731); [S3E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13723); [Kimera-Multi field paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04362v1)). A new testbed has no operating history, so the adoption route is closed to SCooP-Bed.

**Wireless testbeds prove fidelity by twinning.** They run the same experiment over the air and in emulation, then report how similar the KPI time series are. Colosseum-as-digital-twin reports normalised cross-correlation of **0.987 for throughput and 0.982 for SINR** against the Arena indoor testbed. An earlier version reported "92.5% throughput / 80% SINR accuracy" ([Villa et al., TMC 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17063); [NU news](https://insi.northeastern.edu/news/colosseum-as-a-digital-twin-bridging-real-world-experimentation-and-wireless-network-emulation/)). The authors also concede that sounding checks the emulator against its input model, not against the room itself. **Trace-replay systems use the strictest version of this test.** Mahimahi reports a **12.4% median page-load-time error** against the live Internet (secondary source, verify) ([Mahimahi](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc15/atc15-paper-netravali.pdf); [CS244 reproduction](https://reproducingnetworkresearch.wordpress.com/2017/06/05/cs244-17-mahimahi-accurate-record-and-replay-for-http/)). Pantheon's calibrated emulators come within **about 17% of real-path throughput and delay on average**, with a per-path table and a leave-one-out check ([Pantheon](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc18/atc18-yan-francis.pdf)).

**Co-simulators and V2X simulators argue fidelity from coupling, not from measurement.** ROS-NetSim's headline is transparent, "tunable in fidelity" coupling of robot and network simulators ([arXiv 2101.10113](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2101.10113)). It never compares its simulated links with measured ones. Its repository was archived in 2022, and BotNet criticises it on scale and on standards compliance ([GitHub](https://github.com/NESTLab/ros-net-sim); [BotNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.13606)). The admitted gaps line up across the literatures. CARLA-based cooperative perception lacks "accurate communication models" ([ms-van3t-CARLA](https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/wons/wons2024/1570976495.pdf)). Offline datasets miss transmission latency ([V2X-ReaLO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10034v1)). Emulators are only as good as their channel model ([Villa et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17063)).

Taken together, a sensing-plus-communication instrument paper at RA-L should combine all three templates. Take the comparison table from robotics datasets. Take the live-vs-twin error number from wireless testbeds and trace replay. Add a "lessons learned" section, as Kimera-Multi and Team CERBERUS do ([arXiv 2304.04362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04362v1); [arXiv 2207.04914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04914)).

## Every collaborative-perception benchmark uses a channel SCooP-Bed can test

The official configurations, read directly from the authors' repositories, show that every major collaborative-perception method models the channel synthetically. Delay is either constant or i.i.d., and it is quantized to whole sensor frames. None ties delay to message size through a measured, time-varying goodput. None replays outages from traces.

| Method (venue) | Delay model (as configured) | Bandwidth / size | Loss / outage | Pose noise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V2X-ViT ([ECCV 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10638); [repo](https://github.com/DerrickXuNu/v2x-vit)) | `async_mode: 'sim'`, `async_overhead: 100` → constant 100 ms. `'real'` mode = U(0,100) + `data_size` 1.06 Mb / `transmission_speed` 27 Mbps (≈39 ms) + `backbone_delay` 10 ms. | Li et al., lossy V2V ([arXiv 2212.08273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08273)) | — | — | Random corruption of feature maps (unverified; code repo is README-only, [V2VLC](https://github.com/jinlong17/V2VLC)) | — |

In V2X-ViT's paper, the Noisy Setting costs less than 5% AP@0.5, and pose sweeps run to σ_xyz = 0.5 m and 1.0° heading ([arXiv 2203.10638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.10638)). For Where2comm, the only latency curve is a third party's: AP@0.5 falls from 0.7629 at 0 ms to 0.4322 at 400 ms ([CoRA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13191)).

These settings explain the authors' own "synthetic channels: 100%" figure. **Under the frame-quantized models, any delay below 100 ms counts as zero.** Every message therefore "arrives" within its LiDAR frame by construction.

The scale of SCooP-Bed's messages makes the gap concrete. A 1.5 MB message is 12 Mb, about **11× V2X-ViT's 1.06 Mb**. Delivering it inside a 100 ms frame takes about **120 Mbps of sustained goodput**. Against that, the measured link delivers in-frame **86% of the time with one robot and 59–68% with two**. That two-robot drop is the effect i.i.d. per-agent models cannot express. My inference is that it comes from airtime contention at the single shared AP: two robots compete for the same channel, so their delays are correlated, not independent draws. The RTT result points the same way. Ping RTT rises from **5–10 ms idle to 25–33 ms under iperf load**, so delay depends on offered load. A constant `async_overhead` or a Binomial frame lag cannot represent that dependence.

One more opening follows from Where2comm. Under s/G(t) replay, its sparsity threshold changes *latency* as well as volume. Its own paper never measures that trade-off, and SCooP-Bed can show it directly.

## CooperScene took "measured link beside sensors"; SCooP-Bed must win on validated replay

CooperScene is the competitor that changes the framing. It records synchronized C-V2X latency, throughput, packet-loss rate and jitter for every scene, alongside LiDAR, camera and GNSS-RTK. It benchmarks perception under the measured link against an unlimited-network baseline. It states almost SCooP-Bed's message: practical communication "raises awareness of the significant challenges in balancing model efficacy and communication efficiency" ([CooperScene](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31219); [project page](https://cisl.ucr.edu/CooperScene)). Its V2VNet latency estimates, reported as 64,349 ms and 107,432 ms, suggest it already converts message size and measured throughput into delay. That is a version of s/G ([Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2606.31219)). So "first to measure the link alongside the sensors" is no longer safe as a headline. "Real links hurt collaborative perception" is not new either.

The defensible differences are below. Rows marked "unknown" must be checked against CooperScene's full text before any "first" claim.

| Axis | CooperScene (2026) | SCooP-Bed | Strength of differentiation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replay validated against live runs | Not reported in retrieved material | E3 Twin Run | **Strongest.** This is the trace-replay field's standard, and no CP dataset found reports it |
| Per-message continuous-time arrival with outage drops | Unknown; appears to use aggregate size/throughput estimates | d(t) + s/G(t) per message, drops during outages | Strong if CooperScene's mechanics confirm it uses aggregates |
| One-way delay from synchronized clocks | PTP sync; one-way vs RTT unknown | ≤0.99 ms measured offset | Moderate; depends on the 12 ms bound (see risks) |
| PHY-layer channel | Network KPIs only (no CSI found) | Nexmon CSI at 104–141 Hz, RSSI 5 Hz | Strong for ISAC readers |
| Link geometry in the map frame | Not reported | Fresnel clearance through the anchored map (E2) | **Unique**, and only possible because of the one-frame design |
| Probe rate | Unknown | Ping at 100 Hz | Cannot compare yet |
| Ground truth | GNSS-RTK + ICP, about 0.2 m average RMSE | 3.1 cm median, no GNSS or mocap | Different metrics (RMSE vs median) and settings; state with care |
| Scale and labels | 59K frames, 344K boxes | Smaller | **Do not compete on this** |
| Setting and radio | Outdoor, 3 CAVs + 1 RSU, C-V2X sidelink | Indoor, heterogeneous robots + pushcart + masts, shared Wi-Fi AP | Complementary. Shared-AP contention is a regime CooperScene does not cover |

Other neighbours are easier to separate from. V2X-ReaLO evaluates online, but its latency appears to be injected rather than logged ([arXiv 2503.10034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.10034)). DeepSense 6G pairs sensors with PHY beams for beam prediction, not with application-level timing ([DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/deepsense-6g-large-scale-real-world-multi-modal-sensing-and-communication-dataset)). Colosseum validates KPIs for static SDR nodes, with no perception workload ([Villa et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.17063)). ROS-NetSim simulates the links it would need SCooP-Bed's traces to validate ([arXiv 2101.10113](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2101.10113)). The positioning sentence that survives all of them: wireless twins validate KPIs, not perception. Cooperative-perception datasets have real sensors but synthetic links, or, in CooperScene's case, measured links with replay that has not been validated. Robot-network co-simulators have no measured ground truth.

## The recommended highlight and the evidence each claim needs

**Recommended single highlight:** *SCooP-Bed logs every inter-agent Wi-Fi link (RSSI, one-way delay, goodput, CSI) on the same clock and anchored map frame as the robots' sensors, and replays it into collaborative perception message by message, with live-vs-replay error of X%. Synthetic channels used by V2X-ViT-style benchmarks deliver every 1.5 MB message within a LiDAR frame; the measured link delivers 86% with one robot and 59–68% with two.*

Weaker candidate highlights fail on specific grounds. "Heterogeneous agents" is CoPeD's claim. "Ground truth without mocap" has precedent in CoPeD's GPS+SLAM, Kimera-Multi's reference trajectories and S3E's UWB. "Real links hurt" is already CooperScene's claim. "Map predicts radio" is the most original science, but at ρ = 0.60 it is a supporting result, not a headline. A validated replay is the only claim that turns the logs into an instrument other groups can reuse, and that matches how the replay literature earns trust.

The three supporting claims and their evidence:

|---|---|---|---|

Report per-message arrival-time error (median, p95), difference in in-deadline share, and AP difference. Four risks deserve explicit treatment, because a careful reviewer will raise them.

**First, the 12 ms worst-case clock bound exceeds idle one-way delay.** Idle one-way delay is roughly half of a 5–10 ms RTT. Under the bound, d(t) is therefore unidentifiable from clocks alone. The paper should rest one-way-delay claims on the measured ≤0.99 ms offset, or fall back to RTT/2 and say so. Noble's model already derives its parameters from round trips ([Noble et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/263105.263140)).

**Second, measuring G(t) changes the link.** iperf raises RTT about 3–5×, which is the Saturator critique in miniature ([NeuralEmu](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26080)). If d(t) is measured during iperf load, it already contains self-inflicted queueing, and adding s/G(t) on top risks counting the same delay twice. Probe-off control runs, plus a stated rule for which d(t) is used, are needed.

**Third, trace replay cannot react to the tested load.** This is exactly where Wi-Fi replay is known to break ([arXiv 2002.03905](https://arxiv.org/html/2002.03905)), and Pantheon's Wi-Fi path showed the larger error ([Pantheon](https://fyy.cs.illinois.edu/documents/pantheon-atc18.pdf)). E3 should report where replay fails, for example when replayed message rates differ from the recorded load, rather than hide it.

**Fourth, the fluid s/G model has no queue between back-to-back messages.** The paper must state whether consecutive messages share G(t).

## Conclusion

The literature splits along a line SCooP-Bed can straddle. Robotics testbeds earn trust through adoption or dataset completeness. Network testbeds earn it through a live-vs-twin error number. No collaborative-perception dataset, CooperScene included as far as the notes show, has yet reported the second kind of evidence. That makes E3 the paper's most important experiment, not one of five. The synthetic-gap results (E1/E5) explain why the instrument matters. The map-meets-radio result (E2) shows what only a one-clock, one-frame design can reveal. E3 is what proves the instrument works. If E3 slips, the honest fallback headline is "synchronised completeness plus the measured gap", but CooperScene has already taken most of that ground.

The sharpest new point from this synthesis is that SCooP-Bed's own numbers expose a mechanism, not just a deficit. The fall from 86% to 59–68% in-frame delivery when a second robot joins one AP is correlated, load-dependent contention. The i.i.d., frame-quantized channels in V2X-ViT, SyncNet and CoBEVFlow cannot represent it, even when fitted to the measured delays. A best-fit-synthetic baseline that still fails would show that the field needs measured links, not merely better-tuned noise. Making that argument credible requires the same rigour turned on the instrument itself: probe-off controls, a stated one-way-delay rule given the 12 ms bound, and published replay failure cases.
