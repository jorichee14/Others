\section{Experiments: Is SCooP-iT a Trustworthy Instrument?}
\label{sec:experiments}
Five experiments answer the five trust questions of
Sec.~\ref{sec:introduction}, and together they are the evidence
behind the specification sheet of Table~\ref{tab:spec}. Each opens
with its question and its pass criterion, fixed before the data were
read, gives the answer the recordings in hand support, and states what
remains to be collected. Unless stated otherwise, clock and link
numbers come from the 24 September session at site 1 (MIRC 1F): the
survey pass \texttt{survey\_1}, in which one agent owns the channel,
and the cooperation pass \texttt{coop\_2}, in which both robots move and
contend. The reference map is the 15 October mapping pass, cross-checked
by the 24 September one.

\subsection{E1 Headroom: The Testbed Is Finer Than What It Measures}
\emph{Question.} Is the testbed finer than the effects it is meant to
measure? \emph{Pass.} Every testbed error term is at least ten times
below the smallest fusion breaking point.

On one log axis in metres (Fig.~\ref{fig:headroom}), each error term
of the testbed is plotted: clock offset and sniffer bias multiplied by
the 95th-percentile agent speed, extrinsic residuals, held-out board
residual, and second-pass agreement. Against them stand the points at
which collaborative fusion breaks: about \SI{0.2}{\metre} of pose
error, at which late fusion falls below ego-only in the OPV2V study,
and latency expressed as displacement, speed times delay, relative to
an indoor object of \SIrange{0.5}{0.7}{\metre}; the \SI{100}{\milli\second}
figure at which every fusion method falls below ego-only was measured
at vehicle speed and is stated here as displacement. \emph{Answer now.} On \texttt{coop\_2} the measured clock offset is
at most \SI{0.99}{\milli\second}, which is \SI{1}{\milli\metre} at
\SI{1}{\metre\per\second}; the strict bound of \SI{12}{\milli\second}
is \SI{1.2}{\centi\metre}. The RGB-D cart sits in the LiDAR frame to
\SI{3.1}{\centi\metre} median against held-out boards; the LiDAR
robot's mapping trajectory agrees with an independent refinement
(scan-to-map against bundle adjustment) to \SI{1.3}{\centi\metre} and
its map reproduces an earlier map to \SI{1.06}{\centi\metre}; the board
detector reproduces the pipeline to \SI{8}{\milli\metre}. Each
term is judged against the breaking point of its own kind: length terms
against the \SI{0.2}{\metre} pose point, time terms against one
LiDAR frame at \SI{1}{\metre\per\second}, \SI{0.1}{\metre}. The
measured clock offset is 101$\times$ below its point; map refinement,
map repeatability, and the board detector are 15$\times$, 19$\times$,
and 24$\times$ below theirs. Two terms fall short of 10$\times$ and are
reported as such: the cart's \SI{3.1}{\centi\metre} is 6.5$\times$
below the pose point, and the strict \SI{12}{\milli\second} chrony
bound is 8.3$\times$ below one frame, which is why the paper rests the
clock guarantee on the measured offset and carries the bound only as a
worst case. \emph{Remaining.} The LiDAR robot's board
residual on \texttt{coop\_2} (tool exists, not yet run), the radar
reflector residuals, the sniffer bias, and the measured p95 agent
speed that fixes the latency displacement; with those, the final
margin per term.

\subsection{E2 Map Meets Radio: Two Independent References Agree}
\emph{Question.} Are the poses and the link logs right, if neither is
checked by hand? \emph{Pass.} Pooled Spearman $\rho$ between clearance
and the residual signal above 0.3, and a clear-minus-obstructed drop
above \SI{5}{\decibel}, on at least one link kind.

For every link window, the first-Fresnel-zone clearance of the path
between the two ends is computed through the anchored map; a link is
conventionally free-space when at least $0.6\,r_1$ of the first
Fresnel radius $r_1 = \sqrt{\lambda d_1 d_2 / d}$ is clear
\cite{itup530}, which at \SI{5.75}{\giga\hertz} is \SI{28}{\centi\metre}
at the midpoint of a \SI{6}{\metre} link. The distance trend is
removed from the dB signal with a fixed path-loss exponent and an
offset taken from the clear windows; fixing the exponent matters,
since along a route length and obstruction move together and a free
fit puts the wall loss into the slope. A self-test recovers a planted
\SI{12}{\decibel} wall loss as \SI{11.9}{\decibel} ($\rho = 0.68$).
Neither the map nor the poses used the signal, and the signal did not
use the map, so agreement validates both at once; prior
sensing--communication datasets infer blockage from received power
\cite{deepsense6g}, which cannot make this check. Links tested are
the CSI links \texttt{sniffer}--\texttt{mobile\_2} and
\texttt{sniffer}--\texttt{infra\_1}, and the agent-to-AP links from
status RSSI and goodput. \emph{Answer now.} On \texttt{coop\_2}, robot to robot, the
\texttt{mobile\_2} link gives $\rho = 0.60$ and a
\SI{5.4}{\decibel} drop, which passes both halves of the criterion;
the \texttt{infra\_1} link gives $\rho = 0.23$ and
\SI{4.7}{\decibel}, which passes neither, consistent with a fixed
mast whose path to the moving receiver changes less. Since the poses
and the map produced the clearance and the radio produced the RSSI,
agreement on the first link validates both at once.
\emph{Remaining.} The agent-to-AP links, which need the access
point's position read off the anchored map; pooling over
\texttt{survey\_1} and \texttt{coop\_2} and over sites; a
confidence interval on $\rho$; out-of-run prediction, fitting the
detrending on one pass and scoring another; and CSI power as a second
signal (Fig.~\ref{fig:clearance}).

\subsection{E3 Faithful: Replay and Gym Against Reality}
\emph{Question.} Do the replay and the Gym reproduce what the real
agents sensed, sent, and achieved? Three checks, one per use of the
capture (Sec.~\ref{sec:introduction}). \emph{Pass}, fixed before the
data are read. E3a, replay: agreement of at least 90\,\% on which
receiver frame (\SI{100}{\milli\second}) each message lands in, with
the logged replay clearly closer than the synthetic channel. E3b, the
Gym's world: synthetic scans at the recorded poses lie within the real
scan noise of the map (\SI{3}{\centi\metre} median); fitted dropout
and noise statistics match a held-out session; the link indexed by
place, fitted on one pass, predicts the in-frame share of another pass
to within 10 points; and the change in detection AP that the logged
link causes, against the ideal channel, differs between twin and real
by less than half its real value. E3c, deployment: the success rate of
a Gym-trained policy on the real testbed lies within the interval the
Gym predicted for the same scenario.

\emph{E3a, live against replay.}

Two runs follow the same route, mode, and day. Run A records the link
as usual with iperf and ping. Run B replaces iperf with a payload node
that sends stamped messages at \SI{10}{\hertz} in three sizes: about
\SI{1}{\kilo\byte} (boxes), \SI{100}{\kilo\byte} (features), and
\SIrange{1}{2}{\mega\byte} (raw clouds), over the transport and QoS a
collaborative method would use. Because the clocks are synchronized,
the one-way delay of every message is exact. B's messages are replayed
through A's trace and through the synthetic channel, and the
arrival-time error, frame-assignment agreement, and drop agreement are
reported per size and mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:simgap}, left). If the pass
fails for a size or mode, the fluid model of Eq.~\eqref{eq:replay} is
the limitation and a per-packet trace is the stated fix.
\emph{Answer now.} None: no run yet carries stamped payload messages,
so the replay of Sec.~\ref{sec:replay} is a model whose inputs are
measured but whose output is unvalidated, and the abstract says so.
\emph{Remaining.} One session on the \texttt{survey\_1} route: run
A1 (survey, iperf and ping) and B1 (survey, payload node), then A2 and
B2 in cooperation mode, back to back so that building conditions
match; the payload node and the comparison script; and the per-size,
per-mode table of arrival-time error, frame agreement, and drop
agreement, with the synthetic channel on the same axes.

\emph{E3b, the Gym's world against the real one.} Each part of the
world of Sec.~\ref{sec:gym} is checked against real runs that did not
build it. \emph{Sensors}: the twin drives the recorded paths of
\texttt{coop\_2}; its scans are registered to the map and compared with
the real scans at the same poses, and the dropout and noise models
fitted on one session are compared with another. \emph{Link}: the
position-indexed link is fitted on \texttt{survey\_1} and the
contention mode on \texttt{coop\_2}, then asked to predict a third pass
along that pass's own path. \emph{Task}: one cooperative detector runs
on real and twin data under the ideal and logged channels; the twin's
absolute score is not expected to match, since it carries none of the
reflectance and motion distortion of the real sensors, but it must
reproduce the difference the link makes, in which such errors largely
cancel. \emph{Answer now.} None: the twin tooling exists but has not
yet run in Isaac Sim, and the position-indexed link is not yet built.
\emph{Remaining.} The twin run of \texttt{coop\_2}; the fitted
condition models; the position-indexed link; the coop\_2 labels for the
detector.

\emph{E3c, the Gym's policy on the real testbed.} A policy trained in
the Gym for \texttt{OcclusionHandoff} (Sec.~\ref{sec:gym}) is run on the
real agents in the scenario it was trained for, with the link and
conditions recorded as usual. Its real success rate is set against the
distribution of success rates the Gym predicted for that scenario, and
each real failure is attributed by the same log as in the Gym.
\emph{Answer now.} None. \emph{Remaining.} The trained policy; one
field session; the comparison.

\subsection{E4 Same Knob, Same Link: A Setting Reproduces Its Condition}
\emph{Question.} If the same condition is set on another day, is the
same link obtained? \emph{Pass.} The day-to-day distance between link
distributions is below half the survey-to-cooperation distance, for
RTT and goodput.

The same route and mode are recorded on two days. The Wasserstein (or
KS) distance between their RTT, goodput, and RSSI distributions, and
between RSSI-against-position on a shared map grid, is compared with
the distance between survey and cooperation mode on the same route.
\emph{Answer now.} Only the pose half: a map built four weeks before
\texttt{mapping\_A} agrees with it to \SI{1.06}{\centi\metre} on
walls and $2\times10^{-5}$ in scale, so the frame repeats across days.
The mode half, the distance between \texttt{survey\_1} and
\texttt{coop\_2} on the same route, is computable from the 24
September tables but has not been computed. \emph{Remaining.} The
mode distance from the two existing passes; one repeat of the same
route and modes on another day, which the E3 session provides; and the
table of day distance against mode distance for RTT, goodput, and
RSSI on the shared grid.


\subsection{E5 Sim Gap: What a Synthetic Channel Hides}
\emph{Question.} Why not simulate the link? This is a demonstration
with no pass criterion; the claim checked is that cooperation mode
pushes raw clouds past the one-frame boundary while boxes stay within
it, and that outages exist which synthetic channels do not have.

From the logged ping and iperf data, the arrival delay of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:replay} is computed for messages of \SI{1}{\kilo\byte},
\SI{100}{\kilo\byte}, and \SI{1.5}{\mega\byte}, per load mode, and
the share arriving within one LiDAR frame (\SI{100}{\milli\second}) is
set against the synthetic channels of Where2comm (bandwidth budget, no
latency) and V2X-ViT (constant \SI{100}{\milli\second}) exactly as
published (Fig.~\ref{fig:simgap}, right). \emph{Answer now.} Raw
clouds arrive within the frame in 86\,\% of cases when one robot owns
the channel (\texttt{survey\_1}) and in \SIrange{59}{68}{\percent}
with two robots contending (\texttt{coop\_2}), against 100\,\% under
the synthetic channels; the measured delay depends on offered load,
rising from \SIrange{5}{10}{\milli\second} idle to
\SIrange{25}{33}{\milli\second} under iperf load on 24 September
(\SI{3.3}{\milli\second} to \SIrange{60}{109}{\milli\second} on an
earlier pass at the same site), and an earlier cooperation pass
contains three real outages covering 12\,\% of frames, which no
synthetic channel produces. Judged by its latency, the contended link
is healthy: its loaded round trip of \SIrange{25}{33}{\milli\second}
is at most a third of the frame. The misses come from message size over the
goodput the contended link had, which no latency indicator sees. This
is the gap between communication indicators and task indicators that
\cite{zhang2026mean} describes, measured here on one run. The
two-robot drop is the effect that i.i.d.\ per-agent delay models
cannot express: the robots contend for one access point, so their
delays are correlated. A \SI{1.5}{\mega\byte} message is about
11$\times$ V2X-ViT's configured 1.06\,Mb and needs about
120\,Mbit/s of sustained goodput to land inside a frame; under the
frame-quantized synthetic models any delay below
\SI{100}{\milli\second} counts as zero, which is why they deliver
100\,\%. \emph{Remaining.} A best-fit synthetic baseline, a constant
or Binomial delay fitted to the measured delays, to show that even a
tuned synthetic channel mispredicts in-frame share because outages and
contention are correlated; the same figure over more passes and a
second site; and a probe-off control run to separate the queueing the
measurement itself adds from the queueing the team would face.
Detection-level consequences are reported in \cite{scoop_dataset}.

\subsection{Worth: Is It Unique, and Can Others Build It?}
\label{sec:worth}
The two remaining questions are answered outside the experiments.
Uniqueness is Table~\ref{tab:gap_iotj}: SCooP-iT is the only
platform that is indoor, has mobile and fixed nodes, is referenced
without motion capture or GNSS, and measures every pair's link down to
channel state with a measured clock bound and a per-message replay
path into methods. Reproducibility rests on the
released pipeline, the spec sheet, and the bill of materials
(Table~\ref{tab:bom}).
