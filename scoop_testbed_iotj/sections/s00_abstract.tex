\begin{abstract}
Methods for robots and fixed sensors that share what they perceive are
evaluated on vehicle datasets and simulators, where the wireless link
is ideal or a fixed delay. An indoor team instead shares one contended
access point and has no GNSS to say where an agent was when it sent a
message. We present SCooP-iT, an indoor testbed in which a LiDAR robot,
an RGB-D cart, and camera--radar masts record their sensors and every
inter-agent link on one clock and in one metric frame: link state at
\SI{5}{\hertz}, round-trip time at \SI{100}{\hertz}, goodput in
\SI{4}{\second} windows, and Wi-Fi channel state, with measured clock
offsets below \SI{1}{\milli\second}, and agent poses from a self-built
reference map and fiducial chain, without motion capture or GNSS, whose
scale rangefinder control distances confirm to about
\SI{2}{\centi\metre} RMS. The
logged link is replayed per message into any collaborative method,
including unmodified ROS~2 systems, and SCooP-iT Gym, a digital twin
built from the recordings and checked against held-out passes, generates
scenarios for training methods that are then tested on held-out real
recordings. The instrument is checked against
measurements it never used: most of its error terms lie an order of
magnitude below the errors at which fusion breaks; map-derived Fresnel
clearance predicts the measured signal (Spearman $\rho = 0.60$,
\SI{5.4}{\decibel}); and \todo{replay reproduces live message arrivals
to within one frame in X\,\% of messages, and the twin's scans and link
match held-out passes}. Against a synthetic channel
fitted to the same link's statistics, the logged link \todo{changes the
predicted in-frame delivery of large messages by X points, the best
send-size policy, and the merge time of a collaborative SLAM system},
showing that the link's structure in time and place, not only its
statistics, decides the outcome. SCooP-iT is the acquisition platform of
the SCooP dataset.
\end{abstract}
