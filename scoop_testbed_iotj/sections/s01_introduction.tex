\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{T}{eams} of robots and fixed sensors that share what they
perceive can see beyond the occlusions of any one of them. They
cooperate through collaborative SLAM, which places every agent in one
shared map \cite{swarmslam,doorslam,kimeramulti}, and cooperative
perception, which fuses what the agents observe within it
\cite{where2comm,zhou2022gnnperception}. Both are evaluated on vehicle
datasets and in simulation \cite{opv2v,v2xsim,dairv2x,v2v4real}, where
the link between agents is ideal, a constant delay, or a random drop
\cite{v2xvit,lei2022syncnet,wei2023cobevflow}. The Internet of Robotic
Things treats such agents as nodes of one network
\cite{simoens2018iort}, and recent surveys of networked embodied systems
argue that the network and the task have to be measured together
\cite{zhang2026mean,wen2026embodiedmrs}.

Indoors this is hard to do. The agents share one access point, so their
traffic contends; there is no GNSS to say where an agent was when it
sent a message; and the channel changes with the walls the agents move
past. Measuring what the link does to a collaborative method therefore
needs every agent's sensors and every pair's link on one timeline, a
pose for every agent in one frame, and a way to run a new method against
the link the agents actually had, with evidence that this replay is
faithful.

Existing platforms provide parts of this. IoT and wireless testbeds
measure the radio but score no perception task
\cite{adjih2015iotlab,breen2021powder,bonati2021colosseum}. Multi-robot
field systems run over real links
\cite{ginting2021chord,saboia2022achord,cladera2024mocha}, and the most
recent C-SLAM dataset releases inter-robot throughput and latency
outdoors \cite{planetary_cslam}. Cooperative-perception platforms are
outdoor, GNSS-referenced, and enter the measured link into evaluation as
a scalar delay or a live run
\cite{v2v4real,zimmer2024tumtrafv2x,v2xrealo,cooperscene}. Trace-driven
network emulation replays links for network software
\cite{noble1997trace,netravali2015mahimahi,yan2018pantheon}, and for
Wi-Fi it is accurate only when aggregation and medium access are
modelled \cite{syed2020wifiemu,nemfi}.

This paper presents SCooP-iT, the indoor testbed of SCooP (Sensing and
Communication for Cooperative Perception). Its claim is that a real
indoor team can be captured precisely enough, sensing and communication
on one clock and in one frame, for its logged link to be replayed into
any collaborative method, and that a synthetic channel with the same
statistics does not reproduce it. The
contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A heterogeneous indoor testbed with sensing and
    communication on one clock}: two mobile and three infrastructure
    agents on one access point, every pair's link recorded (link state,
    RTT, goodput, CSI), contention as a controlled setting, measured
    clock offsets below \SI{1}{\milli\second}, and sniffer bias bounded
    by stamped probes (Secs.~\ref{sec:design}, \ref{sec:alignment},
    \ref{sec:link}).
    \item \textbf{A building-scale pose reference without motion capture,
    GNSS, or a survey scanner}: a self-built reference map and a fiducial
    chain for agents without LiDAR, with the map's mesh coloured by
    Gaussian splatting of the recorded images, checked by measurements
    that never entered it (a second pass by the other robot, held-out boards, and
    rangefinder control distances, which agree with the map to about
    \SI{2}{\centi\metre} RMS); the LiDAR robot is placed to
    \SI{10}{\milli\metre} and the RGB-D cart to \SI{3.1}{\centi\metre}
    median (Sec.~\ref{sec:gt}).
    \item \textbf{A replay and a digital twin that let other researchers
    test and train collaborative methods on the recordings}: the replay
    takes the messages a method sends and predicts, from the recorded
    link, when each would have arrived or whether it would have been
    lost, for message-level models and unmodified ROS~2 systems alike,
    and is checked against messages sent live \todo{to within one frame}
    (E3), while a synthetic channel with the same statistics misstates
    the delivery of large messages (E5); the twin, built from the
    recordings, generates new scenarios to train on, with each part
    checked against held-out passes before it trains anything (E6)
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:gym}).
\end{enumerate}
Sec.~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work;
Secs.~\ref{sec:design}--\ref{sec:link} describe the testbed;
Sec.~\ref{sec:gym} describes how others test and train methods on the
recordings, through the replay and the twin; Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}
reports six tests, each with a pass criterion stated before the test was
run; and Sec.~\ref{sec:lessons}
gives lessons and limitations. The sequences and labels recorded on the
testbed are released as the SCooP dataset \cite{scoop_dataset}.
