A testbed is more useful as a place to run one's own method than as a
set of results. SCooP-iT Gym puts the measured link and the measured
building behind the interface that reinforcement-learning and
embodied-agent research already uses: agents reset and step as in
Gymnasium and PettingZoo, a scenario file declares the agents, their
roles, the communication relations, and the link, and every episode
leaves an attribution log. The contract follows Lingjing
\cite{lingjing}, a simulated city for heterogeneous agents with engine-side
adjudication and replayable episodes. What SCooP-iT Gym adds is the
network: Lingjing's messages pass an ideal bus whose cost is a message
count, whereas here every message is late or lost as it was on the
testbed, and its world is measured rather than generated.

\paragraph{Link engine} Every message an agent sends goes through the
replay module of Sec.~\ref{sec:replay}, with any of its channels: ideal,
the synthetic channels of prior benchmarks, a synthetic channel fitted to
the pass, or the logged link. The channel is the only variable between
runs of a policy. A message that misses its deadline is attributed to
one cause: an outage, no measurement at that time, waiting behind the
link's previous message, transmission (size over goodput alone exceeds
the deadline), or latency. A failure of a task is thus traced to the
link, not only to an agent or a message as in \cite{lingjing}.

\paragraph{Two engines} In \emph{replay mode} the observations are the
real recordings and the motion is the recording's, so agents control
communication: what to send, to whom, and at what size. The logged link
is exact here, and E3a is its validation. In \emph{twin mode} the
reference map of Sec.~\ref{sec:gt} becomes a simulator scene: its
signed-distance surface is exported to Isaac Sim \cite{isaacsim} in the
map frame, each robot carries the sensors of Table~\ref{tab:sensors} at
the extrinsics of Table~\ref{tab:calib}, and every object has an exact
box at every stamp. Driven along the recorded paths, the twin is at the
recorded places at the recorded times, so the logged link applies
unchanged, and E3b checks the twin against the real runs. The twin also
renders the views the recordings lack, a sensor stream that fell short of
its rate or a viewpoint no robot took. Under the agents' own motion
the loop from received message to action closes, and the link must be
indexed by place instead of time: per cell of the survey's radio map,
scaled by the contention mode, which E2 supports and an out-of-run check
must validate before closed-loop results are reported.

\paragraph{Tasks} Table~\ref{tab:gym} lists the environments.
\texttt{CommSchedule-v0} runs today: per LiDAR frame, a sender chooses
nothing, boxes (\SI{1}{\kilo\byte}), features (\SI{100}{\kilo\byte}), or
the raw cloud (\SI{1.5}{\mega\byte}) and is paid for what lands within
the frame, minus airtime. Its observation is what a robot can know:
the fate of its own past messages and its link monitor's last round trip
and goodput. Under the ideal and synthetic channels the best policy is
to always send the raw cloud; under the logged link a rule that sends it
only when the monitored goodput would land it within the frame
\todo{raises the in-frame share from X to Y\,\% on \texttt{coop\_2}}.
A policy tuned on a synthetic channel is thus tuned to a link the robots
did not have.
