\section{Lessons and Limitations}
\label{sec:lessons}

\paragraph{The CSI receiver shapes the network.} Commodity CSI extraction
imposed one spatial stream, 802.11ac rather than ax, and
\SI{80}{\mega\hertz} on every transmitter, and aggregation caps the CSI
rate at roughly \SIrange{100}{140}{\hertz} under load. These are
constraints of the measurement, not of the robots, and accompany every
throughput number recorded on the platform.

\paragraph{A second wireless hop adds a bias the clock cannot see.}
Reaching the CSI host over a separate management link introduced the
only clock bias chrony cannot observe; stamped probes bound it, and a
wired connection removes it. \todo{Confirm once the Ethernet change is made.}

\paragraph{Check the SLAM solution's clock before refining it.} The SLAM
poses were stamped \SI{46}{\milli\second} early; uncorrected, this alone
made walls \SI{18.8}{\centi\metre} thick over the run instead of
\SI{12.0}{\centi\metre}.

\paragraph{Sensors fall short of their nominal rate.} Recorded at full
resolution with lossless compression, the stereo camera wrote about one
frame per second instead of 15 in one cooperative session, a shortfall
no driver reported. Rates are logged per stream and missing frames are
flagged in the release.

\paragraph{Limits.} One band, one access point, and one CSI receiver per
site; no 802.11ax or multi-stream operation; the CSI describes overheard
channels, not the data links; the replayed link cannot react to a load larger than the one
recorded; the twin's appearance comes from the mapping session's
images, so lighting that changes between sessions and moving people are
not in it, and camera data from it carries a remaining gap;
and on real data the loop from received message to action is
open, since no agent acts on what it receives.
