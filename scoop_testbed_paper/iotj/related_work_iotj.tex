\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

A robot team that shares perception over Wi-Fi is an IoT system in the
full sense: mobile and fixed nodes sense, compute, and exchange data
over a shared, contended network \cite{simoens2018iort}. Each of the
five communities that study a part of such a system measures its own
part carefully and assumes the others away. We review them in turn and
close with the gap at their intersection.

\subsection{Wireless Communications}
\textit{Channel measurement.} Commodity CSI extraction, first on the
Intel~5300 \cite{halperin2011csitool}, then on Broadcom chips with
Nexmon \cite{nexmon} and across several NIC families with PicoScenes
\cite{jiang2022picoscenes}, turned Wi-Fi into a sensor
\cite{ma2019wifisensing}. Channel-sounding and sensing datasets such as
DICHASUS \cite{dichasus}, ESPARGOS \cite{espargos}, MM-Fi \cite{mmfi},
and DeepSense~6G \cite{deepsense6g} pair CSI or beam power with
positions or other modalities.
\textit{Channel prediction.} Ray-traced digital twins
\cite{hoydis2023sionnart,alkhateeb2023digitaltwin,bostontwin,deepmimo}
and learned radio maps \cite{levie2021radiounet} predict the channel
from a geometry that is surveyed or synthetic.
\textit{Access and timeliness.} The throughput of a contended 802.11
cell is modeled analytically \cite{bianchi2000dcf}, and the freshness
of status updates is captured by the age of information
\cite{kaul2012aoi,yates2021aoi}; task-oriented communication encodes
features for a downstream inference task rather than for
reconstruction \cite{shao2022taskoriented}.
\textit{What this community assumes.} CSI is used to sense a person or
a target, not measured on the links over which a robot team exchanges
its data. Geometry is an input to channel prediction, never an
independently measured reference against which the measured channel is
checked. Contention and freshness are studied under saturated or
modeled traffic, not on perception messages whose deadline is a sensor
frame.

\subsection{Robotics}
\textit{Communication-aware robotics.} Robots plan motion around the
channel \cite{muralidharan2021commaware}: they map signal strength
online \cite{fink2010radiomap} or exploit the directionality of the
received signal to keep a team connected \cite{gil2015adaptive}.
CHORD and ACHORD \cite{ginting2021chord,saboia2022achord} and MOCHA
\cite{cladera2024mocha} share data over intermittent real links in
subterranean and field deployments, and \cite{tian2023resilient}
reports multi-robot SLAM run under real communication.
\textit{Collaborative SLAM} places every robot in one map
\cite{swarmslam,doorslam,kimeramulti} and is scored against a pose
reference.
\textit{Indoor ground truth.} Motion capture is accurate but
room-bound \cite{utias,m2dgr}; ultra-wideband anchors extend coverage
but need surveyed anchors and degrade in non-line-of-sight
\cite{zhao2024util}; total-station points \cite{hilti2022} or surveyed
fiducials \cite{airmuseum} leave odometry to carry the pose between
anchors; registration against a dense prior \cite{subtmrs,lamar}
needs a survey scanner; and SLAM-derived references are validated by
sparse landmark distances \cite{incrowdvi} or by internal consistency
alone \cite{envodat}.
\textit{What this community assumes.} Communication-aware systems run
over real links but score no perception against an independent
reference; collaborative SLAM is scored against a reference but the
link it ran over is not logged with it. Building-scale indoor pose
references need a survey instrument or are not validated
independently.

\subsection{Computing and Networked Systems}
\textit{Edge and cloud.} Cloud robotics \cite{kehoe2015cloud} and edge
computing for autonomous driving \cite{liu2019edgeav} move perception
off the agent and so place the network on the critical path; F-Cooper
\cite{chen2019fcooper} frames cooperative perception as an edge
computing system.
\textit{Middleware.} ROS~2 runs over DDS, Zenoh, or MQTT, whose
latency and throughput over Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and 4G have been
benchmarked \cite{zhang2024ros2middleware}; \cite{blumenkamp2022framework}
provides a framework for real-robot wireless experiments.
\textit{Time.} NTP \cite{mills1991ntp} and sensor-network protocols
such as FTSP \cite{maroti2004ftsp} synchronize distributed clocks.
\textit{Emulation.} Record-and-replay of a measured network is
established for mobile and cellular links
\cite{noble1997trace,winstein2013sprout,netravali2015mahimahi,yan2018pantheon}
and is available in the Linux kernel \cite{hemminger2005netem};
ROS-NetSim \cite{calvofullana2021rosnetsim} and Veins
\cite{sommer2011veins} couple a network simulator with a robot or
traffic simulator.
\textit{What this community assumes.} Edge and middleware studies
measure latency with synthetic payloads or modeled channels. Clock
synchronization is evaluated on its own, not as the bound between a
link log and the sensor data it must be joined to. Trace replay feeds
a transport stream, not a multi-agent pipeline keyed to sensor time,
and the traces carry no pose of their endpoints.

\subsection{Perception}
Cooperative perception fuses raw points \cite{chen2019cooper},
features \cite{chen2019fcooper,wang2020v2vnet}, or selected regions
\cite{where2comm,liu2020when2com} from several agents, and is trained
on simulated or outdoor data \cite{opv2v,v2xsim,dairv2x}. A growing
set of methods targets what the network does to the fused result:
latency \cite{lei2022syncnet,v2xvit}, asynchrony
\cite{wei2023cobevflow}, interruptions and packet loss
\cite{ren2024v2xincop}, and pose error \cite{lu2023coalign}.
\textit{What this community assumes.} These methods are trained and
evaluated against synthetic channels, a constant delay, a Bernoulli
drop, or no latency at all, and against pose error drawn from a
chosen distribution. Neither the delay a message actually had nor the
localization error an agent actually had is available to them on the
same timeline.

\subsection{Testbeds and Platforms}
\textit{Wireless and IoT testbeds.} FIT IoT-LAB \cite{adjih2015iotlab}
offers thousands of low-power nodes and over a hundred mobile robots
indoors; POWDER \cite{breen2021powder} and COSMOS
\cite{raychaudhuri2020cosmos} provide city-scale software-defined
radio and edge computing; Colosseum \cite{bonati2021colosseum}
emulates the channel in hardware.
\textit{Multi-robot testbeds} such as the Robotarium
\cite{wilson2021robotarium}, Duckietown \cite{paull2017duckietown},
the CPM Lab \cite{kloock2021cpmlab}, and ChoiRbot
\cite{testa2021choirbot} give positions from motion capture or
overhead cameras.
\textit{Cooperative-perception platforms.} V2V4Real \cite{v2v4real},
V2X-Real \cite{v2xreal}, and TUMTraf-V2X \cite{zimmer2024tumtrafv2x}
provide RTK-referenced multi-agent data; \cite{hawlader2024v2xexp}
reports V2X experiments on real hardware; V2X-ReaLO \cite{v2xrealo}
runs online fusion over a live Wi-Fi link; CooperScene
\cite{cooperscene} releases per-scene C-V2X latency, throughput, and
loss; and CoPeD \cite{coped}, the one indoor heterogeneous team,
reports the estimator's own pose and no link.
\textit{What this community assumes.} Wireless testbeds treat the
radio as the object of study and run no perception task; multi-robot
testbeds treat the network as ideal; cooperative-perception platforms
are outdoor, rely on GNSS, and log the link, if at all, above the
transport layer and without channel state.

\subsection{The Gap at the Intersection}
Table~\ref{tab:gap_iotj} places the closest works on eight
capabilities, grouped by the discipline that needs them, and
Table~\ref{tab:gap_fill} states each discipline's gap, what SCooP-iT
provides, and where the paper shows it. No platform in
Table~\ref{tab:gap_iotj} fills more than one discipline's columns
completely. The wireless testbeds measure the link but not the task;
the perception platforms score the task but simulate the link; the
networked-robot systems run over real links but score nothing; and
none states how well its link log and its sensor data line up in time.
SCooP-iT is built at this intersection: an indoor, heterogeneous team
that records per-pair link and channel state on the sensors' clock,
has a validated pose for every scan without motion capture or GNSS,
and replays the recorded link into any collaborative method.
