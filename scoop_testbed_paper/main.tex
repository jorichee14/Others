\begin{abstract}
Collaboration between robots is useful only if a neighbor's message
arrives in time and lands in the right place. Indoors, the wireless
link is shared and contended, and there is no GNSS against which to
check where a message landed, yet existing multi-robot testbeds treat
the network as ideal, communication-aware systems do not score
perception against ground truth, and cooperative-perception platforms
are outdoor and simulate the link. We present SCooP-Bed, a
heterogeneous indoor testbed of three agent classes, a LiDAR robot, an
RGB-D cart, and camera--radar infrastructure masts, in which the state
of every inter-agent link is recorded on the sensors' clock: passive
link state at \SI{5}{\hertz}, round-trip time at \SI{100}{\hertz},
goodput in \SI{4}{\second} windows, and channel state information at
\SIrange{104}{141}{\hertz}. All agents share one clock, bounded to
\unsure{about \SI{70}{\micro\second}}, and one metric frame at building
scale, obtained without motion capture or GNSS from a self-built
reference map and a fiducial chain for agents without LiDAR, and
validated by a second mapping pass (\SI{1.06}{\centi\metre} surface
agreement), rangefinder control distances, and held-out fiducials. A
channel-in-the-loop replay module runs any collaborative method against
the link the agents actually had. Experiments show that the testbed is
one to three orders of magnitude finer than the effects it measures,
that its map predicts the signal it logs although neither produced the
other, \todo{that replay reproduces live arrivals}, that a load mode
reproduces its link on another day, and that under contention the
delay moves from about \SI{3}{\milli\second} to
\SIrange{60}{109}{\milli\second}, crossing one LiDAR frame, which the
synthetic channels of prior benchmarks do not capture. SCooP-Bed is
the acquisition platform of the SCooP dataset \cite{scoop_dataset}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
A robot team cooperates through two processes, collaborative SLAM,
which places every robot in one shared map \cite{swarmslam,doorslam,
kimeramulti}, and collaborative perception, which fuses what the agents
observe within it \cite{where2comm,zhou2022gnnperception}. Both depend
on a wireless link, and both are useful only if a neighbor's message
arrives before the scene changes and can be placed in the receiver's
frame with accuracy comparable to its own sensors. Indoors, where
occlusion is dense and cooperation matters most, the link is shared
among the agents and contended, and there is no GNSS to say where a
message landed. Measuring what the link does to a team therefore
requires a platform in which the link is recorded alongside the
sensors, and in which every agent has a reference pose on that same
timeline.

No existing platform provides this. Multi-robot testbeds such as the
Robotarium \cite{pickem2017robotarium,wilson2021robotarium},
Duckietown \cite{paull2017duckietown}, the CPM Lab
\cite{kloock2021cpmlab}, and ChoiRbot \cite{testa2021choirbot} give
precise positions from motion capture or overhead cameras but treat
the network as ideal. Communication-aware systems such as ROS-NetSim
\cite{calvofullana2021rosnetsim}, CHORD and ACHORD
\cite{ginting2021chord,saboia2022achord}, and MOCHA
\cite{cladera2024mocha} model or survive the link but do not score
perception against ground truth. Cooperative-perception platforms such
as V2V4Real \cite{v2v4real}, V2X-Real \cite{v2xreal}, TUMTraf-V2X
\cite{zimmer2024tumtrafv2x}, and CoPeD \cite{coped} are outdoor or
GNSS-referenced, and they idealize or simulate the link.

This paper presents SCooP-Bed, a testbed built so that any
collaborative method can be run against the link the agents actually
had, and scored against a pose reference that exists for every scan of
every agent. Its contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A heterogeneous testbed of three agent classes} in
    which every pair's link state and channel state is recorded on the
    sensors' clock: passive state at \SI{5}{\hertz}, RTT at
    \SI{100}{\hertz}, goodput in \SI{4}{\second} windows, and CSI at
    \SIrange{104}{141}{\hertz}, with contention as a controlled knob
    (Secs.~\ref{sec:design}, \ref{sec:link}).
    \item \textbf{A procedure for building-scale ground truth without
    motion capture or GNSS}: a self-built reference map, plus a fiducial
    chain for agents without LiDAR, validated by measurements that never
    enter it, to \SI{1.06}{\centi\metre} surface agreement across
    independent passes, on one clock bounded to \unsure{about
    \SI{70}{\micro\second}} (Secs.~\ref{sec:alignment},
    \ref{sec:gt}).
    \item \textbf{A channel-in-the-loop replay} that runs any
    collaborative method against the recorded link, \todo{validated
    against live execution to within one receiver frame}
    (Sec.~\ref{sec:replay}).
\end{enumerate}
Under contention, the measured delay moves from about
\SI{3}{\milli\second} to \SIrange{60}{109}{\milli\second}, which
crosses one LiDAR frame. Only a testbed that records the link can show
which messages cross it. Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments} establishes, with
five experiments, that SCooP-Bed is precise, consistent,
\todo{faithful}, repeatable, and necessary. The testbed releases no
sequences, labels, or leaderboard; those belong to the SCooP dataset
\cite{scoop_dataset}, which was recorded on it.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Multi-Robot Testbeds and Platforms}
Remotely accessible and educational testbeds made multi-robot
experiments reproducible: the Robotarium
\cite{pickem2017robotarium,wilson2021robotarium}, Pheeno
\cite{wilson2016pheeno}, Duckietown \cite{paull2017duckietown},
Crazyswarm \cite{preiss2017crazyswarm}, the CPM Lab
\cite{kloock2021cpmlab}, the Cambridge minicar fleet
\cite{hyldmar2019fleet}, and the ROS~2 toolboxes ChoiRbot
\cite{testa2021choirbot} and CrazyChoir \cite{pichierri2023crazychoir};
see \cite{mokhtarian2025survey} for a survey. They lead with
reproducibility, a bill of materials, and a validated position
reference, which we adopt. In every case the reference is motion
capture or an overhead camera, confining the arena to one room, and
the network between robots is assumed ideal or is not a measured
quantity.

\subsection{Communication in Multi-Robot Systems}
Communication-aware systems bring the network into the loop.
ROS-NetSim \cite{calvofullana2021rosnetsim} couples a robot simulator
with a network simulator; CHORD and ACHORD
\cite{ginting2021chord,saboia2022achord} and MOCHA
\cite{cladera2024mocha} share data over intermittent real links in
underground and field deployments; \cite{blumenkamp2022framework}
provides a framework for real-robot wireless experiments; and
\cite{tian2023resilient} reports a resilient multi-robot SLAM system
run under real communication, followed by lessons learned. Large-scale
multi-robot SLAM systems report the volume exchanged
\cite{chang2022lamp2,swarmslam,doorslam}. These works survive or model
the link; none records it per pair on the sensors' clock in a form
that can be replayed into another method, and none scores perception
against an independent reference.

\subsection{Cooperative-Perception Platforms and Measured Links}
Cooperative-perception platforms provide multi-agent sensing with
labels: CoPeD \cite{coped}, a heterogeneous air--ground team, is the
closest RA-L neighbor and the one this testbed must be separated from,
since it reports the estimator's own pose and no link; V2V4Real
\cite{v2v4real}, V2X-Real \cite{v2xreal}, TUMTraf-V2X
\cite{zimmer2024tumtrafv2x}, and RCooper \cite{hao2024rcooper} are
outdoor and RTK-referenced; Providentia \cite{krammer2022providentia}
and the mobile-plus-static system of \cite{jimenez2011integrated} are
the closest architectures in combining infrastructure and mobile
sensors. The link has begun to be measured for driving: AutoCast
\cite{qiu2022autocast} and EMP \cite{zhang2021emp} schedule
transmissions under modeled or emulated V2X, \cite{hawlader2024v2xexp}
reports V2X experiments on real hardware, V2X-ReaLO \cite{v2xrealo}
evaluates online fusion over a live Wi-Fi link, and CooperScene
\cite{cooperscene} releases per-scene C-V2X latency, throughput, and
loss traces. All are outdoor, GNSS-referenced, and record the link at
the transport level without channel state.
Table~\ref{tab:capability} compares the capabilities. Only SCooP-Bed
fills every column; CooperScene is the nearest case and differs in
setting, pose reference, CSI, and replay.

\subsection{Indoor Ground Truth Without a Survey Instrument}
\label{sec:gt_related}
Indoor references differ in where the geometry comes from and whether
it is validated independently. Motion capture is accurate but
room-bound \cite{utias,m2dgr}. Sparse anchoring from total-station
points \cite{hilti2022} or surveyed fiducials \cite{airmuseum} leaves
odometry to carry the pose between anchors, and at its limit provides
trajectories only inside a short mocap segment \cite{s3e}. Map
registration against a dense prior yields full, independently
validated trajectories at building scale \cite{subtmrs,lamar}, but the
prior comes from a survey scanner. SLAM-derived references dispense
with the instrument and are validated by sparse measured landmark
distances in the single-agent case \cite{incrowdvi} or by internal
consistency alone in the multi-robot case \cite{envodat}; independent
traversals can converge to the same wrong geometry in featureless
corridors. SCooP-Bed combines an offline SLAM-derived map, scan-to-map
registration, and map-posed fiducials, and validates the result against
control distances and repeated passes that never enter it
(Sec.~\ref{sec:gt}).

\subsection{Network}
All agents associate to a single access point on channel 149 (VHT-80,
\SI{5745}{\mega\hertz}); no ad hoc or mesh mode is used, and power
save is disabled on all transmitters. The transmitter settings are
imposed by the CSI receiver: a 1$\times$1 802.11ac configuration.
802.11ax is disabled, since the receiver cannot demodulate HE frames
and would capture only control frames, and the number of spatial
streams is fixed to one, since a single-antenna receiver cannot
separate a two-stream transmission. The measured single-stream
capacity is \numrange{275}{282}\,Mbit/s uplink and
\numrange{598}{676}\,Mbit/s downlink per mobile agent.

\subsection{Design Decisions}
\label{sec:decisions}
Four decisions make SCooP-Bed a testbed rather than a hardware list.

\emph{The link is measured, not used to carry sensor data.} Each unit
records locally; no sensor stream traverses the wireless network
before reaching disk. Measurement and transport stay separate, so any
method's traffic can be replayed afterwards (Sec.~\ref{sec:replay})
without the recording having been shaped by one method. The only
traffic the testbed itself places on the link is clock synchronization
and the active probes of Sec.~\ref{sec:link}, present in every run and
therefore part of the channel that is measured.

\emph{Single stream, 802.11ax off.} The 1$\times$1 CSI receiver
requires it. It also keeps capacity governed by geometry rather than
by scattering richness, which two-stream capacity depends on and which
a map does not capture.

\emph{Survey and cooperation modes.} In survey mode each agent owns
the whole channel; in cooperation mode several agents run their active
tests at the same time by design. Contention becomes a controlled knob
instead of an accident, and its effect is measurable: RTT moves from
\SI{3.3}{\milli\second} idle to \SIrange{60}{109}{\milli\second} under
load (Sec.~\ref{sec:link}).

\emph{Fixed sensor rigs.} In each agent a fixed rig compounds the
sensors (Fig.~\ref{fig:platforms}), so calibration is done once and
each extrinsic can be validated on observations that did not enter it
(Sec.~\ref{sec:alignment}). The sensors of each platform are expressed
in that platform's origin frame: \texttt{base\_link} for
\texttt{mobile\_1}, so that poses relate to wheel odometry and control;
the LiDAR for \texttt{mobile\_2}, which has no robot body; and the
camera for the masts, which carry no LiDAR.

The remaining knobs a user controls are the number and class of
agents, the infrastructure placement, the trajectory paradigm
(concurrent, intersecting, or rendezvous), and agent speed.

\subsection{Recording}
\label{sec:recording}
Each unit records one bag containing what its sensors and interfaces
produce, and nothing else (Table~\ref{tab:topics}): Ouster packets
with the sensor's metadata message, which allows lossless re-decoding
through the vendor driver at any later time; images and IMU samples;
radar detections; the full on-board visual SLAM output of
\texttt{mobile\_2}, including the drifting visual-odometry path, the
loop-closed path, and the tracker status; the wireless and clock topics
of Sec.~\ref{sec:link} on the same timeline as the sensor topics; and
the raw Nexmon CSI frames on \texttt{sniffer}. Decoded clouds,
reference poses, and parsed CSI do not exist at recording time; they
are produced by the post-processing pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataflow}),
which decodes the streams, computes the reference poses
(Sec.~\ref{sec:gt}), parses the link and CSI logs, and merges all units
into one MCAP per run on the common timebase, with each unit's original
stamp preserved in the MCAP publish time. Every stage is scripted and
released so that the products can be regenerated.

\section{Time and Space Alignment}
\label{sec:alignment}
This section answers how well the testbed puts every measurement from
every agent on one clock and in one rig frame. Each quantity is
validated with data that was not used in its estimation.

\subsection{Clock}
\label{sec:clock}
The clocks are synchronized with chrony. \texttt{mobile\_1} acts as
the server, and every other agent and \texttt{sniffer} act as clients
polling every \SI{8}{\second}. This traffic passes over the measured
link, but at a few hundred bytes per poll it is negligible, and since
it is present in every run it forms part of the measured channel.
Recording starts only after every unit reports a converged offset.
Each unit logs its offset and any clock adjustment
(\texttt{ntp/status}, \texttt{ntp/events}) into its own bag, so the
alignment at any frame can be checked and poorly aligned frames can
be filtered out. Table~\ref{tab:ntp} summarizes the result over
\todo{1} sequences. No clock was stepped during any recording, and the
worst pairwise offset is \todo{about \SI{70}{\micro\second};
reconcile with the table maximum of \SI{42.5}{\micro\second} and with

\subsection{Sniffer Bias}
\label{sec:sniffer}
These offsets describe the stability of each clock relative to the
server, not its accuracy. NTP assumes a symmetric path delay, and any
asymmetry appears as a constant bias that chrony cannot observe. This
is relevant only for \texttt{sniffer}, which reaches the server over
its \SI{2.4}{\giga\hertz} management link with a path delay several
times larger than that of the other units, so its bias may be as large
as half that delay, \SI{7.8}{\milli\second}. We measure the bias
directly from the probe packets that \texttt{mobile\_2} sends and
\texttt{sniffer} captures. Each packet is stamped at transmission on
the clock of \texttt{mobile\_2} and at reception on the clock of
\texttt{sniffer}. Since the packet is in flight for only nanoseconds,
the difference between the two stamps equals the bias of the sniffer
plus its driver latency: the measured link calibrates the clock. The
median difference over a run is \todo{}, which bounds the bias.

\subsection{Error Budget}
\label{sec:budget}
Agreeing clocks are not sufficient if the stamps themselves are late.
Every message is stamped on arrival at its host, so each stamp lags
its measurement by the transport latency of that sensor
\todo{estimates per sensor: LiDAR over Ethernet, cameras over USB,
radar over UART}, and camera stamps lag further by the exposure time.
These lags are approximately constant for a given sensor; we leave the
stamps as recorded so that users can apply their own correction.

Every timing error $\Delta t$ displaces a measurement taken on a
platform moving at speed $v$ by
\begin{equation}
  \delta x = v \, \Delta t .
  \label{eq:time_bound}
\end{equation}
We take $v_{\max} = \SI{3}{\metre\per\second}$ as an upper bound on
agent speed. The clock offset of \unsure{\SI{70}{\micro\second}} then
corresponds to \unsure{\SI{0.21}{\milli\metre}}, and the sniffer bias
of \todo{} to \todo{}, which is \todo{}\,\% of the half-wavelength
$\lambda/2 = \SI{26.1}{\milli\metre}$ at \SI{5745}{\mega\hertz} that
sets the CSI sampling requirement (Sec.~\ref{sec:csi}). Transport
latency and exposure are larger: a constant lag of
\SI{10}{\milli\second} corresponds to \SI{3}{\centi\metre}, but since
it is constant it can be estimated and removed. The largest term is
the LiDAR sweep, \SI{100}{\milli\second} at \SI{10}{\hertz}, so the
first and last points of a scan can be \SI{30}{\centi\metre} apart;
it is removed by per-point deskewing with IMU-propagated motion in the
reference pipeline \cite{glim}. Combining the terms that remain after
compensation, the worst-case intra-frame spatial error at $v_{\max}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \delta x_{\max} = v_{\max}\,\big(\Delta t_{\mathrm{clk}}
  + \Delta t_{\mathrm{lat}} + \Delta t_{\mathrm{exp}}\big)
  = \todo{Y}\,\si{\centi\metre},
  \label{eq:fusion_bound}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_{\mathrm{clk}}$ is the worst clock offset,
$\Delta t_{\mathrm{lat}}$ the largest uncorrected transport latency,
and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{exp}}$ the longest camera exposure. The budget
is dominated by sensor physics and transport, not by synchronization:
clock alignment contributes well under a millimetre, while the larger
terms are either compensated or constant and removable. \todo{Show the
budget as a stacked bar; it feeds the headroom figure of E1.}

\subsection{Extrinsics}
\label{sec:spatial}
Three transforms are estimated per platform. On \texttt{mobile\_1},
\Ttransform{Base}{LiDAR} is obtained by hand-eye calibration: the
robot is driven through a sequence of motions, and the transform is
solved from the pairs of relative motions reported by wheel odometry
and by LiDAR odometry \cite{furrer2018handeye}. On both mobile agents,
\Ttransform{LiDAR}{Camera} is obtained by direct, target-free alignment
of image and point-cloud features \cite{koide2023lidarcam}. For the
radar, whose returns are too sparse for feature alignment,
\Ttransform{LiDAR}{Radar} is obtained with a corner reflector placed at
several positions \cite{domhof2019calib}. For the infrastructure rig,
the camera and radar are mounted on the same fixed bracket and each is
calibrated against a LiDAR by the same methods, which gives
\Ttransform{Camera}{Radar} directly; the world pose of each mast camera
is obtained through the fiducial chain of Sec.~\ref{sec:fiducials}.

Each extrinsic is checked on held-out data. For
\Ttransform{LiDAR}{Camera}, LiDAR returns on the ChArUco boards of the
mapping session are projected into the image and compared with the
detected corners; for the RGB-D agent, whose trajectory uses board
sightings as constraints, the boards are split once per site into an
estimation set and a held-out set, and the residual is evaluated on
the held-out set only \todo{state the split}. For
\Ttransform{LiDAR}{Radar} and \Ttransform{Camera}{Radar}, the reflector
is placed at positions not used in calibration and the radar detection
is compared with the LiDAR centroid. For \Ttransform{Base}{LiDAR}, the
hand-eye result is checked through the board residual of
Sec.~\ref{sec:gt_val}, which tests \Ttransform{Base}{LiDAR} and
\Ttransform{LiDAR}{Camera} at once. Table~\ref{tab:calib} lists the
residuals; the same values are stored with the data
(\texttt{calibration/residuals.json}) so that users can weight each
transform by its accuracy.

\section{Ground Truth Without Motion Capture}
\label{sec:gt}
This section answers how accurately the testbed knows where every
agent is, at building scale, using only onboard sensors. The reference
map is SLAM-derived, which removes the survey scanner normally required
for map registration; every scan is registered against it, so the pose
is carried by the map and not by odometry between anchors; and
fiducial boards posed in the map frame anchor the RGB-D cart and the
masts where registration alone is weak. Fig.~\ref{fig:refpipeline}
shows the pipeline.

\input{figures/refpipeline}

\subsection{Reference Map}
\label{sec:map}
The reference map is built in a dedicated mapping session and held
fixed afterwards; no recorded run contributes to it. A second,
separate mapping pass is recorded and processed independently and is
used only to cross-check the first. A LiDAR-inertial SLAM solution
\cite{glim} estimates the mapping trajectory, with sub-mapping and
loop closure tuned for offline accuracy and checked so that no submap
is left without a long-range constraint. The map is then frozen and
every scan is re-registered into it by point-to-plane ICP against
local plane fits, iterated until the pose corrections fall below the
sensor noise. This follows the prior-map registration used by
survey-grade datasets \cite{newercollege,oxfordspires,lamar}, with the
prior built by our own sensors. Plane fits are used instead of
nearest-neighbour targets because, on a surface smeared by accumulated
drift, a \SI{3.0}{\centi\metre} scan offset produces only
\SI{0.5}{\centi\metre} of nearest-neighbour residual, so the error
would not be visible to conventional ICP.

The frozen map is the metric backbone of every run. LiDAR agents are
localized by scan-to-map registration. For \texttt{mobile\_2}, depth
registration, visual odometry, and a subset of the board sightings of
Sec.~\ref{sec:fiducials} are combined in one pose-graph optimization
per run, with the selected boards entering as absolute pose factors;
the remaining boards are held out for validation. The LiDAR agents
need no board factors, so for them every board sighting is a
validation measurement. All trajectories therefore share one metric
frame by construction, inter-agent relative poses reduce to two
registration errors against shared local geometry instead of two
accumulated drifts, and since no run contributes to the map, each
agent's agreement with it is an out-of-sample check.

\input{figures/refmap}

\subsection{Fiducial Chain for Agents Without LiDAR}
\label{sec:fiducials}
A sensor-constrained agent cannot enter the map by registration alone:
a cold-start ICP against a building-scale map may converge in a wrong
basin, and between registrations visual odometry accumulates drift,
fastest in feature-poor regions. We therefore deploy a network of
printed ChArUco boards, placed mainly in feature-poor regions where
both visual tracking and depth registration are weakest. The boards
are not surveyed: the pose of each is estimated in the reference frame
from the observations of the mapping session itself, so the network
inherits the metric accuracy of the map. This is sparse anchoring as
in \cite{airmuseum}, with the anchors posed by the map instead of by
survey equipment. During the mapping session the mapping agent stops
at each board to record several views; with the mapping trajectory
\Ttransform{World}{LiDAR} and the extrinsic
\Ttransform{LiDAR}{MobileCamera}, the pose of each board is
\begin{equation}
   \Ttransform{World}{Board} = \Ttransform{World}{LiDAR} \times \Ttransform{LiDAR}{MobileCamera} \times \Ttransform{MobileCamera}{Board} .
   \label{eq:board_chain}
\end{equation}
One board serves as the origin of the world frame, and every mobile
agent starts at a board, so that each run begins at a known pose.
Infrastructure masts are placed in the same frame through a board shown
simultaneously to a mobile and an infrastructure camera:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \Ttransform{World}{InfraCamera} = {}
    & \Ttransform{World}{LiDAR} \times \Ttransform{LiDAR}{MobileCamera} \times \\
    &  (\Ttransform{Board}{MobileCamera})^{-1} \times \Ttransform{Board}{InfraCamera} .
\end{split}
\label{eq:infra_camera_chain}
\end{equation}
Board sightings thus serve as initialization at the start of a run, as
drift bounds along it, and as validation of every subsequent run.

\subsection{Validation}
\label{sec:gt_val}
Convergence of the refinement establishes self-consistency, not
accuracy: the plane targets are themselves estimated from the map, so
a self-consistent deformation would converge equally well. Accuracy is
therefore established by four checks that did not enter the
estimation (Table~\ref{tab:gt}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gt_errors}).

\paragraph{Second mapping pass.} Surfaces are measured by their
thickness, the p95--p5 spread of points about planes fitted to
\SI{0.25}{\metre} patches, against the sensor noise floor from
individual sweeps, which carry no pose error. After refinement, floors
are within \SI{1}{\centi\metre} of that floor; walls, observed at
longer range and shallower incidence, remain above it, and more so in
single-visit corridor sections where no revisit constrains lateral
placement. The second pass, captured four weeks later and processed
independently, reproduces the surface placement to
\SI{1.06}{\centi\metre} on walls, with relative scale agreeing to
$2\times10^{-5}$. Since both runs share the sensor and the algorithm,
this establishes scale stability, not absolute scale.

deformation would act, which closes the gap left by the second pass.

\subsection{Scoring Capability}
Because every agent has a pose in the map frame at every scan, an
object labelled once in that frame projects into every agent's frame
through these poses, with the same position, size, and identity across
agents. This is how the testbed scores collaborative perception
without per-frame annotation, and it is why the reference is built to
annotation grade and not only as an evaluation target. The labels
themselves are a release of the dataset \cite{scoop_dataset}.

\section{Link Instrumentation}
\label{sec:link}
This section answers what the testbed observes of each link, how
often, and within what limits. Fig.~\ref{fig:linktrace} shows one run.
\todo{Extend the figure with RTT and CSI amplitude against time
alongside the agents' positions.}

\subsection{Link State}
Passive metrics are read at \SI{5}{\hertz} from the kernel wireless
interface: RSSI, negotiated bitrate and MCS, transmit retries and
failures, and channel busy ratio. During outages the monitor continues
publishing with \texttt{associated=false}, so coverage gaps appear as
explicit labels and not as missing samples. Active metrics are
obtained with \texttt{iperf3} \cite{iperf3} in \SI{4}{\second} tests,
uplink and downlink separately, in survey or cooperation mode
(Sec.~\ref{sec:decisions}); in cooperation mode the measured goodput
includes the contention a deployed team would face. Latency is
measured as ICMP round-trip time at \SI{100}{\hertz}:
\SI{3.3}{\milli\second} when idle and \SIrange{60}{109}{\milli\second}
under load, the latter caused by queueing and not by path loss.

\subsection{Channel State}
\label{sec:csi}
CSI is extracted with Nexmon \cite{nexmon} on a BCM43455c0 in monitor
mode at \SI{80}{\mega\hertz}, which yields one estimate over 242 used
subcarriers per received frame from its VHT-LTF. The extractor,
\texttt{sniffer}, is carried on \texttt{mobile\_1} with a
\SI{2.4}{\giga\hertz} management link kept off the measured band; it
captures the frames of \texttt{mobile\_2} and \texttt{infra\_1}. Only
QoS data frames give full-bandwidth estimates; control frames are
captured for PHY diagnosis but excluded from analysis, since mixing
them changes the effective bandwidth from frame to frame. The achieved
data-frame rates were \SI{141}{\hertz} from \texttt{infra\_1} and
\SI{104}{\hertz} from \texttt{mobile\_2}.

The channel varies over roughly half a wavelength of motion,
$\lambda/2 = \SI{26.1}{\milli\metre}$ at \SI{5745}{\mega\hertz}. If
three estimates per half-wavelength are required to follow these
variations, the achieved rates are sufficient up to a relative speed
of about \SI{1.2}{\metre\per\second}; above that the CSI is too sparse
to resolve them. The rate cannot simply be raised by sending more
packets, since Wi-Fi aggregates many packets into one transmission and
each transmission yields a single channel estimate from its preamble;
disabling aggregation would raise the CSI rate but distort the
throughput being measured. CSI phase is not coherent across devices,
which run on independent oscillators, so each transmitter--receiver
pair is treated as an independent sensing link.

\section{Channel-in-the-Loop Replay}
\label{sec:replay}
This section answers whether any collaborative method can be run
against the link the agents actually had.

\paragraph{Model.} A message of size $s$ sent at time $t$ over link
$(a,b)$ arrives at
\begin{equation}
  t + d_{ab}(t) + s / G_{ab}(t),
  \label{eq:replay}
\end{equation}
where $d_{ab}(t)$ is the one-way delay and $G_{ab}(t)$ the most recent
goodput measured on that link, and is dropped when the link was down
(\texttt{associated=false}, or a run of lost pings). Because the
clocks agree to \unsure{about \SI{70}{\micro\second}}, $d_{ab}$ can be
measured directly from stamped probes where they exist, instead of
being assumed to be $\mathrm{RTT}/2$; only a synchronized testbed can
do this. The ideal channel (instant, lossless), the synthetic channels
of prior benchmarks (a fixed bandwidth budget and a fixed or sampled
delay \cite{where2comm,v2xvit,lei2022syncnet,wei2023cobevflow}), and
the logged channel all pass through one module
(Fig.~\ref{fig:replay}), so the channel is the only variable between
runs of a method.

\paragraph{Interface.} Any method that emits (sender, receiver, $t$,
size) plugs in and receives, for each message, an arrival time or a
drop. The implementation extends an OpenCOOD-style wrapper that already
turns latency into staleness and drops collaborators, with a schedule
driven by the trace; a converter places recorded frames in the layout
it reads. \todo{Name the released modules.}

\paragraph{Positioning.} Trace-driven network emulation replays a link
for network software, from trace modulation \cite{noble1997trace}
through netem \cite{hemminger2005netem}, Cellsim
\cite{winstein2013sprout}, Mahimahi \cite{netravali2015mahimahi}, and
Pantheon \cite{yan2018pantheon}; robotics and network co-simulators
couple a robot simulator with a simulated network
\cite{calvofullana2021rosnetsim,sommer2011veins,xu2021opencda};
collaborative-perception benchmarks simulate a link for perception.
SCooP-Bed replays the measured robot link into perception, on the same
clock as the sensors. The fluid model of Eq.~\eqref{eq:replay} is
chosen because the iperf windows give goodput, not per-packet delivery
opportunities as in Mahimahi; E3 measures how much accuracy the simpler
model loses.

\section{Experiments: Is SCooP-Bed a Trustworthy Instrument?}
\label{sec:experiments}
A testbed paper is judged on whether readers can trust it as an
instrument. Five experiments each back one property of that trust, and
together they are the evidence behind the specification sheet of
Table~\ref{tab:spec}: E1 shows the testbed is precise, E2 that it is
consistent, E3 that its replay is faithful, E4 that its settings are
repeatable, and E5 that what it measures could not have come from
simulation. Each opens with its question and pass criterion and closes
with the result. All recordings are from three indoor sites on the
Guangfu Campus of National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University.

\subsection{E1 Headroom: The Testbed Is Finer Than What It Measures}
\emph{Question.} Is the testbed finer than the effects it is meant to

\subsection{E2 Map Meets Radio: Two Independent References Agree}
\emph{Question.} Are the poses and the link logs right, if neither is
checked by hand? \emph{Pass.} Pooled Spearman $\rho$ between clearance
and the residual signal above 0.3, and a clear-minus-obstructed drop
above \SI{5}{\decibel}, on at least one link kind.

For every link window, the first-Fresnel-zone clearance of the path
between the two ends is computed through the anchored map; a link is
conventionally free-space when at least $0.6\,r_1$ of the first
Fresnel radius $r_1 = \sqrt{\lambda d_1 d_2 / d}$ is clear
\cite{itup530}, which at \SI{5.75}{\giga\hertz} is \SI{28}{\centi\metre}
at the midpoint of a \SI{6}{\metre} link. The distance trend is
removed from the dB signal with a fixed path-loss exponent and an
offset taken from the clear windows; fixing the exponent matters,
since along a route length and obstruction move together and a free
fit puts the wall loss into the slope. A self-test recovers a planted
\SI{12}{\decibel} wall loss as \SI{11.9}{\decibel} ($\rho = 0.68$).
Neither the map nor the poses used the signal, and the signal did not
use the map, so agreement validates both at once; prior
sensing--communication datasets infer blockage from received power
\cite{deepsense6g}, which cannot make this check. Links tested are
the CSI links \texttt{sniffer}--\texttt{mobile\_2} and
\texttt{sniffer}--\texttt{infra\_1}, and the agent-to-AP links from
status RSSI and goodput. \emph{Result so far} (coop\_2, robot to
robot): $\rho = 0.60$ and a \SI{5.4}{\decibel} drop on the
\texttt{mobile\_2} link, which passes; $\rho = 0.23$ and
\SI{4.7}{\decibel} on the \texttt{infra\_1} link. \todo{Pool over
passes and add the agent-to-AP links once the AP position is in
\texttt{ap.yaml}; Fig.~\ref{fig:clearance}.}

\subsection{E3 Twin Run: Replay Matches the Real Link}
\emph{Question.} Does replaying a logged link reproduce what the real

Two runs follow the same route, mode, and day. Run A records the link
as usual with iperf and ping. Run B replaces iperf with a payload node

\subsection{E5 Sim Gap: What a Synthetic Channel Hides}
\emph{Question.} Why not simulate the link? This is a demonstration
with no pass criterion; the claim checked is that cooperation mode
pushes raw clouds past the one-frame boundary while boxes stay within
it, and that outages exist which synthetic channels do not have.

From the logged ping and iperf data, the arrival delay of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:replay} is computed for messages of \SI{1}{\kilo\byte},
\SI{100}{\kilo\byte}, and \SI{1.5}{\mega\byte}, per load mode, and
the share arriving within one LiDAR frame (\SI{100}{\milli\second}) is
set against the synthetic channels of Where2comm (bandwidth budget, no
latency) and V2X-ViT (constant \SI{100}{\milli\second}) exactly as
published (Fig.~\ref{fig:simgap}). \emph{Result so far} (24 Sep): raw

the channel and in \SIrange{59}{68}{\percent} with two robots
contending, against 100\,\% under the synthetic channels; the measured
delay is bimodal, about \SI{3}{\milli\second} idle and
\SIrange{60}{109}{\milli\second} under contention, and coop2\_0828
contains three real outages covering 12\,\% of frames, which no
synthetic channel produces. Detection-level consequences are reported
in \cite{scoop_dataset}.

\section{Lessons Learned and Limitations}
\label{sec:lessons}

\paragraph{CSI constraints shape the network.} Commodity CSI extraction
imposed a single spatial stream, 802.11ac rather than ax, and
\SI{80}{\mega\hertz} on every transmitter, and aggregation caps the
CSI rate at roughly \SIrange{100}{140}{\hertz} under load, sufficient
only below about \SI{1.2}{\metre\per\second}. These are constraints of
the measurement, not of the robots, and must accompany any throughput
number recorded on the platform; raising the CSI rate by disabling
aggregation would distort the throughput being measured.

\paragraph{NTP asymmetry on the sniffer's management link.} Reaching
the CSI host over a second wireless hop introduced the only clock bias
the protocol cannot observe. Probe packets stamped on both ends
measure it; a wired connection to \texttt{mobile\_1} removes it.
\todo{Confirm once the Ethernet change is made.}

\paragraph{Cold-start ICP falls into wrong basins at building scale.}
Hence boards go in feature-poor regions and every run starts at a
board.

\paragraph{Plane-fit refinement versus nearest neighbours.} On a map
smeared by drift, nearest-neighbour ICP hides a \SI{3}{\centi\metre}
scan offset behind a \SI{0.5}{\centi\metre} residual; plane targets
made the error visible and were necessary to reach the reported
surface thickness.

\paragraph{Limits.} One band and one protocol; one access point per
site; one CSI receiver; the self-built map rests on the control
distances for absolute scale, since the two passes share a sensor and
a pipeline; endpoint clearances hide obstacles within
\SI{0.7}{\metre} of a radio. \todo{Add scale: three sites, sessions,
hours, once final.}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
SCooP-Bed gives a heterogeneous indoor robot team one clock, bounded to
\unsure{about \SI{70}{\micro\second}}, one metric frame, validated to
\SI{1.06}{\centi\metre} across independent passes without motion
capture or GNSS, and a record of every inter-agent link from passive
state to channel state, with a replay module that puts that link back
in the loop for any collaborative method \todo{validated to within one
receiver frame}. Under contention the real link moves the delay across
one LiDAR frame, which no synthetic channel of prior benchmarks
reproduces. Next steps are more agents, other radios, multi-antenna
CSI, and a wired CSI host. The sequences recorded on SCooP-Bed, their
labels, and the benchmark are released as the SCooP dataset
\cite{scoop_dataset}.
